# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 208
**Title:** The National Delivery Clipboard
**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister enters the briefing room to find every official holding a clipboard.

No one is writing on them.

"Why does everyone look like they are about to inspect a leisure centre?"

The Permanent Secretary lifts his clipboard half an inch.

"It is the National Delivery Clipboard."

"It is a board with a clip."

"That is the operational basis, yes."

The comms director explains that the clipboard projects readiness without requiring a screen, a password, or a chair.

"The public like visible grip."

"On policy?"

"On paper, initially."

The policy lead hands the Minister a briefing note already clipped to a branded board.

Across the top, in sober navy capitals, it says:

**DELIVERY IS IN HAND.**

The Minister tries to remove the note.

The clip snaps shut on his finger.

The junior official writes down "delivery encountered early resistance".

"Do not minute my hand."

"I minuted it as contact with the frontline."

## ACT ONE

The clipboard began as a prop for a factory visit.

By nine fifteen, it had become a cross-government symbol of disciplined implementation.

The Minister reads the submission.

"Departments are invited to identify opportunities for clip-enabled assurance."

"What is clip-enabled assurance?"

"Assurance," says the Permanent Secretary, "with less flapping."

The comms director has prepared a photo grid of ministers holding clipboards in various sites: a platform, a corridor, a warehouse, a school hall, and one place nobody can identify.

"Where is that?"

"Generic delivery environment."

"It is a car park."

"A car park is a delivery environment if you look determined in it."

Legal asks whether the phrase "in hand" creates an implied obligation to hold something at all times.

The policy lead suggests a handling protocol.

The Permanent Secretary suggests a Handling Protocol Board.

The Minister says that a board about boards may attract comment.

The comms director says that is why it will meet without photographs.

## ACT TWO

Departments begin adapting the clipboard to their own priorities.

Transport wants a clipboard that can survive delays.

Health asks whether wipe-clean clipboards count as reform.

Education proposes a smaller version for younger stakeholders.

The Treasury asks if existing walls can be designated vertical clipboards to reduce procurement pressure.

The policy lead unveils the Clipboard Confidence Ladder.

Step one is holding.

Step two is nodding while holding.

Step three is pointing at clipped material.

Step four is field-based grip.

The Minister points at step four.

"That is just going outside."

"With governance."

The comms director receives an urgent message.

A photograph has emerged of a senior figure holding a clipboard upside down during a site visit.

The press office wants to know whether this should be framed as transparency.

"How is upside down transparency?"

"It shows there is no hidden text."

The Permanent Secretary says the important thing is not whether the clipboard was readable, but whether it conveyed purposeful custody.

"Custody of what?"

"The page, Minister."

## ACT THREE

An emergency delivery huddle is called to decide whether clipboards should remain visible at the afternoon announcement.

Everyone brings a clipboard.

The table is too crowded for the actual announcement.

The junior official tries stacking them by department, but the clips hook together and form a single administrative raft.

The Minister looks at it.

"Is that our policy?"

"It is our policy estate," says the Permanent Secretary.

The comms director proposes a reset line: clipboards are not the policy, they are the posture.

"Why do we need a posture?"

"Because a position can be challenged."

Facilities reports that several clipboards are missing.

The room stiffens.

"Missing or redeployed?"

"One is in the kitchen holding the dishwasher rota."

The policy lead says that could be a useful proof of concept.

The Minister says it proves only that people already had things to clip.

For a moment, this sounds dangerously like evaluation.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon announcement begins in a training suite arranged to look like an operations centre.

Behind the Minister, officials hold clipboards at identical angles.

A reporter asks whether the government is announcing a new delivery plan or a new way of holding old ones.

The comms director makes a small rotating gesture, meaning "bridge".

The Minister looks at his clipboard.

The top sheet has slid down, revealing the dishwasher rota.

He reads the first line before anyone can stop him.

"Mugs are everyone's responsibility."

The room goes quiet.

The Permanent Secretary looks oddly moved.

The Minister lowers the clipboard.

"That may be the clearest implementation principle we have produced all week."

A reporter asks if that is now government policy.

"No," says the Minister. "It is a reminder that if everyone holds the board, someone still has to wash the mugs."

The comms director mouths the word "usable" with cautious surprise.

## TAG

Back in the briefing room, the branded clipboards are being inventoried.

The Permanent Secretary says they will be retained for future visible grip events.

The Minister asks what happened to the one from the kitchen.

The junior official holds it up.

It now says:

**DELIVERY IS IN HANDWASH.**

The comms director asks whether that can be softened to "rinse-ready".
