# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 182
**Title:** The National Clarity Stapler
**Date:** 2026-08-21

## COLD OPEN

The press office is trying to find the official line on a new "clarity drive", but every version has been stapled to a different briefing note.

The Minister arrives holding three sheets of paper joined at odd angles.

"Is this the announcement?"

The Permanent Secretary studies it.

"Technically, Minister, that is the announcement, the rebuttal, and the internal warning not to announce the announcement."

The comms director, already on a headset, mouths the word "smile" with the haunted urgency of someone defusing an appliance.

The Minister smiles.

The press office groans.

On the wall, a newly printed poster reads:

**NATIONAL CLARITY WEEK: ONE MESSAGE, MANY VERSIONS**

Someone has stapled it upside down.

## ACT ONE

The clarity drive has been created to reassure the public that the government is speaking plainly.

The problem is that the plain-English version has failed legal clearance because it can be understood.

In the strategy room, aides arrange draft statements by risk category.

"This one says what we mean," says a junior official.

"Red folder," says the Permanent Secretary.

"This one says roughly what we mean but with enough distance to survive a committee."

"Amber folder."

"This one says we welcome the opportunity to engage with stakeholders on future-facing delivery principles."

"Ministerial folder."

The Minister wants a decisive phrase.

"Can I say we are cutting through confusion?"

The comms director checks the grid.

"You can say you are reducing the ambient complexity environment."

"That sounds worse."

"It tests better with people who don't know what it means."

Meanwhile, a new instruction arrives from the centre: every department must appoint a Clarity Champion by lunchtime.

No one wants the role because the first duty is explaining what it is.

## ACT TWO

The first stakeholder roundtable collapses when the agenda contains an item marked "simplify agenda".

The business representative asks whether the clarity drive will reduce paperwork.

The policy lead says yes, then adds that the reduction will be measured through a new monthly paperwork impact return.

The charity representative asks for a single point of contact.

The department offers a shared inbox, an escalation inbox, a clarification inbox, and a shadow inbox used only when the clarification inbox becomes unclear.

The Minister tries to recover.

"The public want government to speak like normal people."

There is a silence.

The Permanent Secretary leans in.

"We prefer 'accessible operational language communities'."

The comms director receives breaking news: another department has already launched a "Straight Answers Portal".

The portal consists of one page saying "updates will follow".

It is being praised online for brevity.

The Minister demands something equally clear.

Within eleven minutes, the team produces a rival product: the National Clarity Stapler, a symbolic office device that will "join policy to delivery".

Nobody can find an actual stapler.

## ACT THREE

The launch is moved to a community advice centre because it has fluorescent lighting and looks sincere.

A lectern is positioned in front of a noticeboard full of genuinely useful information, which the advance team covers with branding.

The Minister rehearses the key line:

"Today, we staple clarity to the national conversation."

The comms director says it needs to sound less stationery-based.

"Today, we fasten understanding to delivery."

The Permanent Secretary nods.

"Very robust."

Five minutes before the cameras roll, the single printed speech falls apart because someone used a paperclip in a stapler photo opportunity.

A special adviser starts reading from the version control log.

"Minister, your opening paragraph is either 'families deserve plain answers' or 'plain families deserve answerable delivery', depending on whether legal cleared the verb."

The Minister picks the shorter one.

The press pack asks whether the clarity drive has a budget.

The Minister says the budget is clear.

"So how much is it?"

"Clearly allocated."

"Allocated where?"

"Within the overall envelope of clarity."

The clip goes live before the launch ends.

## CLIMAX

Back at the department, the hashtag has turned into a game: people are posting photographs of confusing signs, broken forms, and government webpages that ask users to print a digital declaration.

The comms director wants to pivot.

"We say the public response proves demand for clarity."

The Permanent Secretary suggests a review.

"We cannot have a clarity review," says the Minister. "That sounds like we reviewed clarity and found it missing."

"Then a clarity refresh."

"That sounds like it went stale."

"A clarity reset."

"That sounds like we broke it."

The junior official, still holding the unstapled speech, quietly suggests doing the useful thing: publish one short page with the actual changes, the actual dates, and who to contact.

Everyone looks at them.

The Minister considers it.

"Could we announce that?"

The room erupts.

"No," says the comms director. "If we announce the simple page before we publish it, we need a holding line. If we publish it before we announce it, we need a discovery strategy. If we do both together, we need confidence."

The Permanent Secretary finally finds a stapler in a drawer marked "legacy transformation assets".

It is empty.

## TAG

At 6:43 PM, the department publishes the simple page by accident.

It is clear, useful, and short.

For twelve minutes, nothing goes wrong.

Then the Minister's office sends an email asking whether the page can be expanded into a speech, a dashboard, a pilot, and a national roadshow.

The junior official closes the laptop.

Across the office, the upside-down poster falls off the wall.

It lands the right way up.
