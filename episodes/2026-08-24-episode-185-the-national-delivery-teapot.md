# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 185
**Title:** The National Delivery Teapot
**Date:** 2026-08-24

## COLD OPEN

The department has been asked to prove it is delivering.

Not delivery in the old-fashioned sense of things arriving, being finished, or noticeably improving.

Delivery as a public posture.

The Minister enters the briefing room to find a porcelain teapot on the table, surrounded by officials with the solemnity usually reserved for fiscal events and leaking roofs.

"Why is there a teapot?"

The Permanent Secretary folds his hands.

"It is the National Delivery Teapot."

"Of course it is."

"It allows us to demonstrate that delivery is brewing."

The comms director turns the teapot slightly so the slogan faces the cameras:

**POURING PROGRESS INTO COMMUNITIES**

The Minister stares at it.

"Does anything come out?"

"That depends," says the policy lead, "on whether we define steam as output."

## ACT ONE

The National Delivery Teapot began as a prop for a stakeholder roundtable.

The roundtable became a pilot.

The pilot became a cross-cutting initiative.

The initiative became an urgent note to the centre explaining why a teapot had appeared in the forward grid.

The comms director insists the object has warmth.

"People understand tea. Tea says calm, practical, domestic competence."

"Tea also says we have stopped working," says the Minister.

"Only if served."

The Permanent Secretary explains that the teapot is not intended to replace delivery.

"It is a convening artefact."

"For what?"

"Expectation."

A junior official asks whether the department might instead publish the actual delivery milestones, including the delayed ones.

Everyone looks uncomfortable, as if the teapot has heard.

The policy lead says milestones can be polarising.

"A date creates a moral relationship with the future."

"Good," says the Minister. "That is how dates work."

## ACT TWO

By midday, the teapot has stakeholders.

The regional team wants local teapots.

The digital team wants an online teapot that fills when progress is made.

Finance wants to know whether teapots are capital or resource spending.

Legal wants assurance that "pouring progress" does not imply liquid government services.

The comms director has scheduled a photo opportunity with the Minister lifting the lid.

"What will be inside?" asks the Minister.

"Possibility."

"Can possibility be photographed?"

"If the lighting is forgiving."

The Permanent Secretary suggests putting in folded slips of paper, each representing a workstream.

The junior official says each slip could include a named owner and a completion date.

"That is not a slip," says the policy lead. "That is accountability in teapot form."

The room pauses to consider whether this is an opportunity or a threat.

## ACT THREE

The launch takes place in the atrium because the teapot looks too small in the press room and too absurd in the cabinet room.

The Minister stands beside a table, the teapot, and a banner reading:

**DELIVERY YOU CAN FEEL**

The comms director whispers that feel is safer than measure.

The Minister begins.

"Today we are setting out a practical, people-focused approach to getting things done."

The Permanent Secretary nods with the grave expression of a man watching a metaphor leave containment.

The Minister lifts the lid.

Inside are six folded slips.

The first says: "Awaiting alignment."

The second says: "Subject to review."

The third says: "Dependent on dependencies."

The fourth says: "Further detail in due course."

The fifth is blank.

The sixth says: "Ask junior official for the actual milestones."

A camera zooms in.

The comms director coughs so hard it briefly becomes the event soundtrack.

## CLIMAX

Within an hour, the image of the teapot has become a news story about whether the government has confused public administration with tableware.

The Minister is furious.

"How did the actual useful note end up inside the prop?"

The junior official raises a hand.

"Because it was the only item with content."

The Permanent Secretary says the department should not overreact.

"Minister, the important thing is that the story is about delivery."

"The story is about a teapot."

"A delivery teapot."

"That is worse."

The comms director proposes a clarification: the teapot was symbolic of listening.

Legal says listening was not the approved symbolism.

The policy lead suggests renaming it the National Implementation Vessel.

The Minister asks for the milestones.

The junior official opens a plain spreadsheet.

There are eight rows, four amber risks, two red risks, three named blockers, and one completed action that nobody had thought to announce.

The room goes quiet.

"We have delivered something?" asks the Minister.

"Yes."

"Without a teapot?"

"Yes."

The comms director looks wounded.

## TAG

By evening, the completed action has been published.

It receives modest praise for being specific.

The teapot is moved to a side table and reclassified as "internal culture collateral".

The Permanent Secretary commissions a lesson-learning exercise on ceramic risk.

The comms director says the slogan can still be useful if they pivot to "steeping ambition".

No one responds.

The Minister asks the junior official how often the milestone spreadsheet can be updated.

"Daily."

"Daily feels ambitious."

"It is where the information already is."

The Minister looks at the teapot, then at the spreadsheet.

"Fine. We publish the spreadsheet."

The Permanent Secretary reaches for the teapot.

"Shall I pour?"

"Only if something comes out."

He tilts it.

A single slip falls onto the table.

It says: "Do not use props."
