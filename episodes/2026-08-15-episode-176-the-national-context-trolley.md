# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 176
**Title:** The National Context Trolley
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office unveiled the National Context Trolley.

It was a three-shelf office trolley carrying a folder, a whiteboard pen, and six different explanations for why yesterday had been more complicated than it looked.

The Cabinet Secretary said the trolley would help ministers bring context to difficult questions without having to carry any of it personally.

Communications liked the mobility.

Policy liked the word context because it sounded useful without admitting to a position.

Treasury asked whether the trolley had been procured as furniture, strategy, or rolling ambiguity.

Legal said rolling ambiguity was not yet a recognised asset class.

Digital offered to add wheels to the dashboard.

The junior adviser asked what problem the trolley solved.

The Cabinet Secretary pushed it gently across the room.

It drifted into the doorstop and stopped.

Communications said this proved the trolley was grounded.

## ACT ONE

By 08:20, every department wanted a shelf.

Transport requested the top shelf for visible delivery.

Health asked for a lower shelf because it was under pressure.

Education wanted a shelf at child eye level, subject to safeguarding.

Treasury said shelves should be allocated according to fiscal headroom, which meant nobody could put anything on them.

Legal drafted guidance clarifying that items placed on the trolley were not necessarily government policy, unless photographed beside a minister.

Communications asked whether the trolley could appear in the background of interviews as a sign of momentum.

Policy worried that momentum implied direction.

The Cabinet Secretary suggested "portable preparedness."

Everyone wrote it down because it had two words and neither could be checked by lunch.

Facilities arrived to say the trolley squeaked.

Digital said the squeak could be reclassified as user feedback.

The junior adviser said the public might prefer answers to mobile shelving.

The Cabinet Secretary said answers were exactly the sort of thing that needed context.

## ACT TWO

At 10:05, the Prime Minister rehearsed a statement beside the trolley.

"This government is wheeling context closer to the people," the Prime Minister read.

Legal requested that closer be changed to appropriately nearer.

Treasury asked whether the people had agreed to receive context.

Communications wanted the trolley angled so the folders looked full but not incriminating.

Policy added a binder labelled PRACTICAL NEXT STEPS.

The binder was empty.

Policy said that made it adaptable.

Digital taped a QR code to the handle, which opened a page saying the context was being loaded.

The Cabinet Secretary asked how long loading would take.

Digital said the page did not measure time, only expectation.

Facilities returned with a spray can for the squeak.

Legal asked whether lubricating the trolley altered the policy position.

The junior adviser suggested simply answering the expected question.

Communications stared at the trolley.

The trolley stared back with three neutral shelves.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Context Handling Board met to decide what could ride on each shelf.

Top shelf: reassuring facts.

Middle shelf: careful language.

Bottom shelf: things nobody wanted near a microphone.

Treasury asked whether the bottom shelf could be treated as a reserve.

Legal said only if nobody called it hidden.

Communications proposed a clear acrylic cover to show transparency.

Policy asked whether the cover could be translucent.

Digital suggested an interactive shelf map.

Facilities said interactive shelves were called drawers, and drawers were a different procurement category.

The Cabinet Secretary asked if the trolley could carry a drawer in principle.

Legal said in principle was safer than in public.

The junior adviser noticed the PRACTICAL NEXT STEPS binder had slid onto the floor.

Nobody picked it up.

Communications said a fallen binder might humanise the process.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood at the lectern while the National Context Trolley waited just within camera shot.

"This government is bringing portable preparedness to the hard work of delivery," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked what had actually changed.

Communications nodded towards the trolley.

The Cabinet Secretary whispered that the trolley was not authorised to answer independently.

The reporter asked again.

Policy opened the PRACTICAL NEXT STEPS binder and found only a receipt for the trolley.

Treasury asked to see the receipt.

Legal advised against acknowledging the receipt while the receipt was in context.

Digital refreshed the QR page.

It changed from CONTEXT LOADING to CONTEXT AVAILABLE IN PRINCIPLE.

The reporter asked whether in principle meant no.

The trolley began to roll slowly away from the lectern because the floor was not level.

For a moment, the whole room watched context leave.

The junior adviser caught it by the handle and turned it back.

The Prime Minister said this showed the government could redirect context when necessary.

Communications wrote that down as the headline.

## TAG

By evening, the National Context Trolley had been absorbed into a wider Portable Preparedness Programme.

Treasury requested a shelf-by-shelf savings plan.

Legal prepared a disclaimer for slopes.

Digital launched a tracker showing whether context was near, approaching, parked, or available in principle.

Policy suggested a second trolley for lessons learned.

Facilities said there was only one trolley.

The Cabinet Secretary said that created a healthy discipline around context.

The junior adviser asked whether anything had been delivered.

Everyone looked at the trolley.

On the bottom shelf, behind the careful language, the empty binder sat neatly beside the answer.

**END**
