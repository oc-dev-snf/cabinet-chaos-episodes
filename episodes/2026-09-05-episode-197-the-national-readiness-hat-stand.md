# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 197
**Title:** The National Readiness Hat Stand
**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister arrives to find a wooden hat stand in the middle of the briefing room.

It has six hooks, three labels, and one lanyard hanging from the top like a surrender flag.

The largest label reads:

**NATIONAL READINESS HAT STAND**

The Minister stops in the doorway.

"Why is there furniture in the risk meeting?"

The Permanent Secretary looks up from a folder.

"It is not furniture, Minister. It is readiness infrastructure."

"It is a hat stand."

"That is its delivery form."

The comms director is photographing it from a low angle.

"The public want to know who is wearing which hat. This makes accountability visible."

"Nobody is wearing a hat."

"Exactly. That is the gap."

The policy lead enters carrying a box of laminated role cards.

"We have Preparedness, Assurance, Escalation, Listening, and Grip."

The Minister peers into the box.

"Why are there twelve Grip cards?"

"Several departments asked for one without accepting weight."

The junior official appears with a small paper hat labelled Contingency.

"Where should this go?"

Everyone looks at the empty hook marked Later.

## ACT ONE

The hat stand began as a diagram.

The diagram became a slide.

The slide became a workshop.

The workshop became a procurement request because someone wrote "standing capability" in the action log.

Now it is a varnished object in a meeting room, and the Minister is expected to launch it at three.

He reads the briefing note.

"The National Readiness Hat Stand will clarify role ownership across complex delivery environments."

"Good sentence," says comms.

"It sounds like we have run out of cupboards."

The Permanent Secretary offers a measured correction.

"It indicates that functions can be held in readiness."

"On hooks."

"Temporarily."

Finance has already asked whether the hooks count as capital.

Legal has advised that nobody should physically place a hat on Grip unless Grip has been cleared by the relevant owner.

Operations is concerned that the stand blocks the route to the whiteboard.

The policy lead is enthusiastic.

"It is flexible. If priorities change, you move the hat."

The Minister lifts the Listening card from one hook.

The whole stand wobbles.

"Apparently Listening destabilises the structure."

The junior official writes that down, then immediately looks sorry.

## ACT TWO

By midday the stand has acquired a governance framework.

Each hook must have a named senior owner, a supporting deputy, and a clear escalation path if the hat slips.

Nobody knows what hat slipping means.

Comms says it means loss of narrative grip.

Legal says it means nothing and should remain that way.

Finance says if hats are slipping there may be replacement costs.

The Permanent Secretary convenes a Hook Allocation Board.

Preparedness is assigned to Operations.

Assurance is assigned to Legal.

Escalation is assigned to whoever is not in the room.

Listening is assigned jointly to Strategy and Communications, which makes both teams suspicious.

Grip remains unclaimed.

The Minister points at it.

"Why will nobody take Grip?"

"It has consequences," says the policy lead.

"Everything has consequences."

"Yes, but Grip has minutes."

A special adviser suggests replacing Grip with Confidence.

The junior official quietly tests the word on a label maker.

The label comes out too long and curls at the edge.

"That feels about right," says the Minister.

## ACT THREE

The launch line is rewritten six times.

Version one says the government is "putting the right hats on the right hooks".

Version two says "readiness is not a hat, but it must have somewhere to go".

Version three is withdrawn after someone notices it sounds like a lost property policy.

The comms director wants the Minister to stand beside the hat stand and gesture to the empty hooks.

"Empty hooks show capacity."

"They show nobody brought a hat."

"That is a matter of interpretation."

The Permanent Secretary proposes a low-risk visual.

"You could touch the stand briefly."

"Why?"

"To demonstrate contact with readiness."

The policy lead has discovered that two departments have issued competing hook maps.

One puts Escalation above Assurance.

The other puts Assurance above Escalation.

Neither includes Listening.

"Where did Listening go?"

The junior official checks her notes.

"It was moved to make room for Confidence."

The Minister closes his eyes.

"So we lost Listening while trying to display Confidence."

The room becomes professionally quiet.

## CLIMAX

The press room is crowded, because someone has leaked a photograph of the hat stand.

Reporters have already named it.

Comms refuses to say the name out loud.

The Minister stands at the lectern while the stand waits behind him, just visible enough to become the story and not visible enough to explain itself.

A reporter asks whether the government believes readiness can be delivered through office furniture.

The Permanent Secretary studies the carpet.

The comms director makes the smallest possible gesture toward the approved line.

The Minister ignores it.

"No. Readiness is not a display item. It is people knowing what they are responsible for before the room gets hot. It is decisions with owners, plans with dates, and fewer meetings where we argue about where to hang the word Grip."

A reporter asks if the launch is cancelled.

"The prop is cancelled," says the Minister. "The work is overdue."

Another reporter asks who owns the work.

The room behind him pauses.

The Minister looks back at the empty hook.

"I do," he says.

## TAG

Back upstairs, the junior official removes the labels from the hat stand.

The Permanent Secretary asks where it will go.

"Storage," she says.

Comms appears at the door.

"Could we keep it nearby? There is a resilience roundtable on Monday."

The Minister walks past with the Grip card in his hand.

"Only if it holds coats."

The junior official tests one sleeve on the empty hook.

For the first time all day, the stand does something useful.

**END**
