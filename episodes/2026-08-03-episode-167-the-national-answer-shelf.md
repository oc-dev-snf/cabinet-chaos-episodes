# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 167
**Title:** The National Answer Shelf
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Answer Shelf.

It was described as a practical, visible place where the government would keep clear answers until the country was ready to receive them.

Communications said the public wanted answers.

Policy said the public wanted answers in a form compatible with ongoing work.

Treasury asked whether the shelf already existed.

Facilities said there was a shelf in the briefing room, but it was currently holding old confidence.

Legal asked whether placing an answer on a shelf constituted publication, pre-publication, storage, display, or a regrettable metaphor.

Digital asked whether the shelf could sync across departments.

The Cabinet Secretary said the Answer Shelf would end speculation by proving that answers existed in principle.

The first approved shelf labels were:

ANSWER PENDING.

ANSWER IN HAND.

ANSWER SUBJECT TO HANDLING.

ANSWER AVAILABLE AFTER ALIGNMENT.

The junior adviser asked whether an answer on a shelf was still an answer if nobody could use it.

The room agreed that was exactly why the shelf needed a strategy.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had begun sending answers for shelving.

Transport submitted an answer about delays, then asked for it to be placed behind a separate explanation about timetables.

Health submitted an answer in chart form and requested that the y-axis be treated as clinically sensitive.

Education submitted an answer to a question no one had asked, on the grounds that it was the answer they had prepared.

The Home Office submitted a locked box marked ANSWER INSIDE, and said the lock was part of the answer.

Treasury submitted a note saying all shelves must be funded from existing cupboards.

Communications wanted the shelf visible behind the Prime Minister during interviews.

Policy warned that visible answers could create expectations.

Communications said expectations were manageable if photographed from the correct angle.

Legal said the word shelf implied capacity, and capacity implied possible empty space.

The Cabinet Secretary proposed filling unused space with process.

The junior adviser suggested answering one question directly to demonstrate the concept.

There was a short procedural pause while everyone identified the owner of directly.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Answer Shelf went into live rehearsal.

Every press line now required a Question Anticipation Note, an Answer Placement Form, and a Shelf Readiness Assessment.

The Prime Minister practiced the opening statement.

"We are focused on delivering for families," the Prime Minister said.

Communications placed an answer on the shelf.

It read: DETAILS WILL FOLLOW.

Policy said that was more of a corridor than an answer.

Treasury approved corridor because it suggested movement without requiring arrival.

Legal asked whether details had, in fact, agreed to follow.

Digital proposed a status tracker showing each answer as queued, preparing, almost available, or undergoing responsible finalisation.

The Prime Minister tried again.

"People deserve certainty."

The shelf displayed: CERTAINTY IS BEING PRIORITISED.

Communications said it needed more warmth.

Policy changed it to: CERTAINTY IS AT THE HEART OF OUR PRIORITISATION.

The Cabinet Secretary said the shelf was already improving government because everyone could now see which answer was being avoided.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the shelf became crowded.

Transport had added an answer for every station except the one in the reporter's question.

Health had added an answer with three annexes, two caveats, and one compassionate footnote.

Education had added an answer explaining that standards were rising if measured from an appropriately modest floor.

The Home Office had added a second locked box marked CONTEXT FOR LOCK.

Treasury had removed three answers and recorded the removal as streamlined clarity.

The shelf began to sag.

Facilities said this was not a political problem, but it had become load-bearing.

Communications proposed calling it a robust shelf.

Legal warned that robust could be tested.

Policy suggested resilient.

Treasury asked whether resilience was cheaper than robustness.

Digital said the online shelf had crashed because too many people had clicked answer.

The junior adviser asked whether the problem was that the government had made answers visible without making them accessible.

The Cabinet Secretary said accessibility would be considered once visibility had completed its pilot.

## CLIMAX

The press conference began with the Answer Shelf positioned neatly beside the lectern.

"This government believes in clear answers," the Prime Minister said.

The shelf displayed: CLEAR ANSWERS ARE BEING STORED RESPONSIBLY.

A reporter asked when the answers would be released.

Communications signalled readiness.

Policy signalled sequencing.

Treasury signalled affordability.

Legal signalled that release was a word with consequences.

The Prime Minister said the shelf represented a new relationship between government and the public.

The shelf displayed: RELATIONSHIP SUBJECT TO TERMS.

Another reporter asked whether the shelf was just a way of showing people where the answer would be if there was one.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was an unfair description of a developing assurance environment.

Digital announced that future versions would allow users to sort answers by theme, confidence level, and likelihood of becoming a statement.

The junior adviser said the public could now see the government's main achievement: it had moved from not answering questions to curating the absence of answers.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Answer Shelf would bring openness, discipline, and a more orderly national conversation about why certain questions were still under active shelving.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested extra shelving.

Treasury approved one shared bracket, provided every answer was lightweight, reusable, and capable of being deferred indefinitely.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's announcement could promise answers off the shelf.

Legal said only if nobody reached for them.

**END**
