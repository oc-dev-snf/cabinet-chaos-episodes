# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 199
**Title:** The National Implementation Bookmark
**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister arrives to find every folder in the Cabinet room fitted with a red ribbon.

Each ribbon hangs halfway down the page, as if government has paused itself for a difficult sentence.

The largest folder has a brass label:

**NATIONAL IMPLEMENTATION BOOKMARK**

The Minister looks at it.

"Why does the delivery plan have a tassel?"

The Permanent Secretary folds his hands.

"It is a continuity device."

"For reading?"

"For progress."

The comms director is arranging three bookmarks in a fan for the photographer.

"It shows we know where we are."

"It shows we stopped."

"No, Minister. It shows we can resume."

The policy lead enters carrying a velvet tray of departmental bookmarks.

"Health has requested a wider ribbon. Transport wants one with milestones printed on it. Treasury has asked if they can use a paperclip."

The junior official appears with a folder that contains only bookmarks.

"Where do we put the bookmark strategy?"

Everyone looks at the main delivery plan.

Nobody wants to open it.

## ACT ONE

The National Implementation Bookmark began as a note in the margin.

Someone had written "hold place" beside a commitment that had been announced before anyone had agreed the verb.

The phrase moved into a grid, then into a slide, then into a ministerial submission titled "Maintaining Momentum Through Place Retention."

By breakfast it had become stationery.

The Minister reads the launch line.

"The bookmark demonstrates that government is keeping its place in the delivery journey."

"Warm," says comms.

"It says we lost the plot but kept the page."

The Permanent Secretary offers a correction.

"It means the work remains active."

"Does it?"

"Institutionally active."

Legal has advised that the bookmark must not imply an obligation to turn the next page.

Finance has asked whether ribbons are recurring expenditure if they keep being moved.

Operations has warned that several departments are already bookmarking pages they have not read.

The policy lead is optimistic.

"It creates confidence that nothing has been forgotten."

The Minister lifts the ribbon.

Underneath it is a paragraph that begins, "Further work is required to define implementation."

"Good news," he says. "We have bookmarked the absence of implementation."

The junior official writes down "absence may require owner."

## ACT TWO

By midday the bookmark has become a system.

Every delayed decision receives a ribbon.

Every ribbon receives a tracking number.

Every tracking number requires a meeting to confirm that the ribbon has not drifted from the agreed page.

The Permanent Secretary convenes the Place Assurance Board.

Comms says the board should be photographed from above.

"It will look decisive."

"It will look like a desk has gone camping," says the Minister.

Legal objects to the word camping because it suggests intent to remain.

Finance objects to the word desk because nobody has confirmed asset ownership.

The policy lead unveils a dashboard showing bookmark density by department.

One department has achieved full bookmark coverage.

"What does full coverage mean?"

"No unmarked uncertainty."

"And no visible progress."

"That is a presentation challenge."

The junior official has discovered that two teams have bookmarked the same page for opposite reasons.

One believes it marks the next action.

The other believes it marks the point at which action became impossible.

The Minister asks which interpretation is official.

The Permanent Secretary says both are being treated as aligned until the workshop.

## ACT THREE

The afternoon launch is delayed because the bookmark has moved.

Nobody knows who moved it.

This is treated as a security incident until the junior official points out that the folder was carried to the photocopier.

Comms wants to call it a "dynamic resumption marker."

The Minister wants to call it "a thing that fell out."

The compromise is "mobile continuity indicator."

The policy lead has prepared a visual demonstration.

The Minister is supposed to open the plan, indicate the bookmark, and say, "This is where delivery continues."

He practises once.

The bookmark slips down two pages and lands on the risk register.

The room goes silent.

"Is that bad?"

"It depends whether the public can read upside down," says comms.

The Permanent Secretary suggests gluing the bookmark in place.

Legal immediately objects.

"A fixed bookmark could imply the government has chosen not to progress."

"A loose bookmark implies we cannot control stationery."

"That is less justiciable."

The junior official quietly adds "gravity" to the risk register.

## CLIMAX

In the press room, the Minister stands beside the open delivery plan.

The red ribbon lies across the page like a warning line.

The first reporter asks whether the government has mistaken marking a place for moving forward from it.

The comms director looks at the approved words.

The Permanent Secretary looks at the bookmark as if it might answer.

The Minister closes the folder.

"A bookmark is useful if you are actually coming back to the page," he says. "It is not a substitute for reading it. It is not an implementation plan. It is not a milestone. It is a small admission that the work is unfinished, and the honest thing to do with unfinished work is finish it."

A reporter asks whether the National Implementation Bookmark has been cancelled.

"No," says the Minister. "It has been demoted to bookmark."

Another asks where implementation now sits.

The junior official steps forward with a plain sheet of paper.

At the top it says:

**OWNER. ACTION. DATE.**

There is no ribbon.

## TAG

Back in the Cabinet room, the velvet tray has been removed.

The bookmarks are in a mug marked Miscellaneous.

The Permanent Secretary says this is a sensible interim repository.

The Minister opens the delivery plan at page one.

The junior official hands him a pen.

"Where should I put the bookmark?" asks the policy lead.

The Minister reads the first sentence.

"At the end," he says, "when we get there."
