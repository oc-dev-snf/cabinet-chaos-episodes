# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 179
**Title:** The National Decision Mat
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office unveiled the National Decision Mat.

It was a grey rubber rectangle placed outside the main briefing room, with DECISIONS printed across it in calm blue letters.

The Cabinet Secretary explained that all future decisions would now be wiped clean before entering government.

Communications said the mat sent a strong message about grip.

Policy said it created a threshold for action.

Treasury asked whether wiping a decision counted as preventative maintenance.

Legal asked whether a decision placed on a mat had technically arrived.

Digital offered to install sensors so the public could see when decisions were approaching.

The junior adviser asked why decisions needed to stand outside.

The Cabinet Secretary said it was important not to let them track uncertainty into the room.

Everyone looked at the mat.

No decisions came in.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had begun queueing with proposals held carefully at arm's length.

Transport brought a rail timetable covered in sticky notes and asked whether late arrivals could be wiped first.

Health brought a delivery update and asked if the mat could remove legacy context.

Education brought a reform plan and said it had already been lightly shaken in the corridor.

Treasury announced that decisions would receive one wipe each, with additional wiping subject to spending review discipline.

Legal circulated interim guidance confirming that wiping did not imply endorsement, rejection, movement, stasis, or the existence of a cloth.

Communications wanted a launch photograph of ministers standing confidently beside the mat.

Policy warned that standing beside the mat might imply proximity to a decision.

Digital demonstrated the prototype dashboard.

It displayed a spinning icon and the words DECISION FOOTFALL BEING NORMALISED.

The junior adviser pointed out that nobody had put an actual decision on the mat.

The Cabinet Secretary said the mat was still bedding in.

Facilities said it was rubber.

Policy said rubber was consistent with adaptive bedding.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister rehearsed the public line.

"We are getting decisions over the threshold," the Prime Minister read.

Legal asked to replace over with near, to avoid prejudicing the direction of travel.

Treasury asked whether threshold implied capital expenditure.

Communications suggested "a practical doorway to delivery."

Policy preferred "a controlled entry point for outcomes."

The Cabinet Secretary liked entry point because it sounded like movement without guaranteeing admission.

Digital's dashboard updated to show seventeen decisions in the approach zone.

No one knew where the approach zone was.

Facilities said the mat had been moved slightly to stop the door catching.

Legal asked whether that constituted policy relocation.

Treasury asked who had authorised the slightness.

The junior adviser offered to open the door.

Communications said the door should open only when the cameras were ready.

Policy said a closed door created valuable anticipation.

The Prime Minister asked whether anticipation could be announced.

The Cabinet Secretary said it could be trailed.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Decision Hygiene Subcommittee met to classify incoming choices by cleanliness.

Straightforward decisions were suitable for direct wiping.

Complex decisions required a pre-wipe note.

Controversial decisions were to be placed beside the mat until conditions became less tactile.

Urgent decisions could cross the mat in principle, provided they did not leave marks.

Communications asked whether marks could be reframed as evidence of activity.

Legal said evidence was acceptable as long as it was not discoverable.

Treasury asked if the mat could be leased to departments between announcements.

Digital proposed a subscription model for decision analytics.

The Cabinet Secretary said the country needed fewer subscriptions and more confidence.

Digital renamed the subscription Confidence Plus.

Policy produced a laminated flowchart showing the approved route from uncertainty to managed presence.

The flowchart ended at a box marked FURTHER THRESHOLD TO BE CONFIRMED.

The junior adviser asked whether that was just another mat.

Nobody answered, because the possibility was operationally useful.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood in front of the National Decision Mat while officials arranged a stack of folders just behind the door.

"Today we are giving decisions a clear and responsible way into government," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked which decision would enter first.

Communications gestured towards the mat.

The Cabinet Secretary nodded to Policy.

Policy nodded to Legal.

Legal checked whether nodding created a record.

Treasury asked whether the first decision would set an expensive precedent.

Digital refreshed the dashboard.

It changed from DECISIONS APPROACHING to THRESHOLD ENGAGEMENT IN PROGRESS.

The door opened slightly.

A folder slid forward, touched one corner of the mat, and stopped.

Everyone leaned in.

The folder was labelled DRAFT DECISION ON WHETHER TO SELECT FIRST DECISION.

The reporter asked if this was a decision.

Legal said it was a decision-shaped preparation.

Communications said the country could see real movement.

The Prime Minister said responsible government meant never rushing a threshold.

The folder remained on the mat, gathering nothing.

## TAG

By evening, the National Decision Mat had been upgraded to the Decision Entry Programme.

Treasury requested a cost-benefit analysis of each wipe.

Legal prepared guidance on accidental stepping.

Digital launched a public tracker showing decisions as approaching, threshold-adjacent, wipe-ready, paused for texture, or awaiting controlled entry.

Policy proposed a second mat for implementation.

Facilities said the first mat had started curling at the edges.

Communications called it a visual metaphor for ambition.

The Cabinet Secretary said the curl showed the system was responsive to pressure.

The junior adviser asked whether any decision had actually crossed into the room.

Everyone looked down.

The mat was perfectly clean.
