# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 163
**Title:** The National Readiness Corridor
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Readiness Corridor.

It was described as a practical reform to make sure the country was not merely ready, but visibly moving through readiness at a measured pace.

The Cabinet Secretary said the corridor would connect ambition to delivery without forcing either of them to share a room too early.

Communications said the public liked movement.

Policy said movement needed a destination.

Treasury said destinations were expensive.

Legal asked whether the corridor could be described as non-directional.

Digital asked whether the corridor required a live tracker.

The junior adviser asked whether the government was ready for anything specific.

The room agreed specificity was the sort of luxury that belonged near the end of a corridor.

The proposed scheme contained five stages:

ENTRY READINESS.

ACTIVE READINESS.

ADVANCED READINESS.

NEAR-DELIVERY READINESS.

READINESS FOR ANNOUNCEMENT OF DELIVERY.

The Prime Minister said the country deserved a government that was prepared to prepare, ready to become ready, and determined to demonstrate readiness in a way that could survive lunchtime questioning.

Everyone agreed this sounded sufficiently firm if spoken beside a map.

## ACT ONE

By 08:20, departments had been asked to nominate policies suitable for the corridor.

Transport nominated a rail reliability plan that had already missed two preparatory milestones.

Health nominated a waiting list initiative that was waiting for a list of waiting lists.

Education nominated a school improvement package awaiting improvement from the schools package team.

The Home Office nominated a border scheme marked OPERATIONAL, SUBJECT TO OPERATIONAL DEFINITION.

Treasury nominated a spending review annex titled Things We Might Have Been About To Do Anyway.

Communications asked whether any of the policies could be photographed walking.

Policy said metaphorical motion was safer.

Legal said metaphorical motion still required a risk note if anyone put arrows on it.

The Cabinet Secretary unveiled the Readiness Corridor Access Protocol.

Policies could enter the corridor if they had a ministerial quote, a problem statement, and at least one official who knew where the previous version of the plan had gone.

Once inside, each policy would receive a readiness lanyard.

Green meant confidently ready.

Amber meant readiness in development.

Blue meant ready in principle.

Grey meant awaiting colour clearance.

The junior adviser asked whether a corridor with no exit was technically a holding area.

Treasury said holding areas were cheaper than programmes.

Communications said the important thing was that nothing looked stuck.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, Digital demonstrated the National Readiness Dashboard.

Every policy appeared as a small dot moving slowly along a line.

Transport's dot moved backwards, which Digital described as retrospective progress.

Health's dot split into three, which Policy described as integrated complexity.

Education's dot disappeared behind a permissions error.

The Home Office dot refused to disclose its location.

Treasury's dot reached the end immediately because the line had been shortened for fiscal reasons.

Communications asked whether all the dots could face right.

Digital said dots did not face anywhere.

Communications said that was exactly the kind of negativity that made the dashboard feel tired.

The Prime Minister entered for a rehearsal.

"We are moving Britain from planning to delivery," the Prime Minister read.

Legal objected to moving.

Treasury objected to delivery.

Policy objected to Britain being represented by dots without regional sensitivity.

The revised line became: "We are strengthening the conditions in which practical progress can proceed responsibly."

Communications said it had the rhythm of something that could be announced twice.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the corridor jammed.

Transport claimed it could not advance until Health confirmed whether hospital access roads counted as infrastructure.

Health said it could not advance until Education confirmed whether school nurses were workforce or prevention.

Education said it could not advance until Treasury confirmed whether future funding was real funding or illustrative funding.

Treasury said illustrative funding had feelings too, but fewer obligations.

The Cabinet Secretary called an emergency readiness clearance meeting.

Policy proposed a cross-cutting readiness sprint.

Communications proposed calling it a national moment of acceleration.

Legal proposed removing the word acceleration until somebody checked whether anything had sped up.

Digital proposed adding a "paused for quality" status.

The Prime Minister asked whether paused for quality was better than delayed.

Communications said it was delayed wearing a jacket.

A minister suggested announcing that the corridor had already created discipline.

The junior adviser asked whether discipline meant nobody could leave.

Everyone agreed not to put that in the explainer.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon press conference took place in front of a long blue backdrop labelled THE NATIONAL READINESS CORRIDOR.

"Today, we are showing that government is moving with purpose," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked which policy had reached delivery.

Communications signalled "journey".

Policy signalled "conditions".

Legal signalled "avoid endpoints".

The Prime Minister said several important measures had entered advanced readiness, which was a significant step in the responsible sequencing of delivery.

The reporter asked whether that meant anything had happened.

Treasury said happening was only one form of value.

Digital said the dashboard would soon allow the public to see policies entering, progressing, pausing, reframing, awaiting interdepartmental alignment, and preparing for onward readiness.

Another reporter asked whether the corridor was just a way to announce policies before they existed.

The Cabinet Secretary said the corridor ensured that policies existed in a disciplined environment before being exposed to the operational burden of existing outside it.

The junior adviser said the system had not made delivery faster, but it had made delay easier to navigate.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Readiness Corridor would replace confusion with visible preparation, uncertainty with managed sequencing, and inaction with a clear sense of where inaction currently was.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested signs for their readiness corridors.

Treasury approved the signs, provided they pointed in both directions and could be reused for retreats.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's grid could say the government was halfway down the corridor.

Legal said yes, provided nobody asked which way it was facing.

**END**
