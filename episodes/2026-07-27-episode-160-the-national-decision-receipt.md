# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 160
**Title:** The National Decision Receipt
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Decision Receipt.

It was described as a practical new mechanism for proving that decisions had happened, even where outcomes remained in a period of respectful development.

Communications said the public wanted proof.

Policy said proof required a decision.

Legal said a decision required a record.

Treasury said a record required storage.

Digital offered to build a Receipt Portal where every major decision would generate a printable confirmation, a reference number, and a small green tick indicating that something had moved through the system.

The Cabinet Secretary said the scheme would bring discipline to the space between intention and delivery.

The junior adviser asked whether a receipt normally came after buying something.

Treasury said that depended on whether the purchase had been approved, deferred, reprofiled, or described as a strategic acquisition of future optionality.

Communications said receipts were reassuring because they looked final.

Legal said final was not a term to be used near government paper.

The Prime Minister said the country deserved a government that could show its workings, validate its intentions, and issue a clear record of decisive movement.

Everyone agreed this sounded like action, provided nobody checked what had been bought.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had been asked to submit decisions requiring receipts.

Transport submitted a receipt for considering timetable resilience in the context of rain.

Health submitted a receipt for a decision to develop a plan to reduce the number of plans about queues.

Education submitted three receipts for one curriculum announcement, because the first two had been superseded by stakeholder clarity.

The Home Office submitted a receipt marked RECEIVED WITHOUT ADMITTING ARRIVAL.

Treasury submitted a receipt for not spending money yet.

Communications liked that one.

Policy said not spending money was not a decision unless someone had first proposed spending it.

Treasury said proposals were a known risk factor in expenditure.

The Cabinet Secretary introduced the standard receipt format:

DECISION NAME.

DATE OF DECISION-ADJACENT ACTIVITY.

RESPONSIBLE AREA.

EXPECTED OUTCOME CLASS.

PROOF OF SERIOUSNESS.

Legal asked whether seriousness created liability.

Communications asked whether proof of seriousness could be a photo of a minister holding a folder.

Digital said the portal could automatically generate folder images where diaries were too full for actual folders.

The junior adviser asked if the public might prefer the decision itself to the receipt.

The room went quiet, as if a small alarm had sounded in a cupboard.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, Digital demonstrated the Receipt Portal.

DECISION STATUS: RECEIPTED.

IMPLEMENTATION STATUS: TO FOLLOW.

CONFIDENCE STATUS: GENERATED.

PUBLIC ASSURANCE: DOWNLOADABLE.

Communications admired the green tick.

Legal asked whether the tick represented completion, compliance, acknowledgement, or decorative confidence.

Digital changed the tooltip to INDICATIVE POSITIVE MARK.

Treasury asked whether the portal could issue receipts for savings before savings had occurred.

Digital added a function called ANTICIPATED RECEIPTING.

Policy said anticipated receipting sounded like claiming a meal before entering the restaurant.

Treasury said that was unfair, because restaurants had menus.

The Cabinet Secretary suggested a receipt hierarchy:

WHITE RECEIPT: DECISION TAKEN.

BLUE RECEIPT: DECISION TAKEN TO TAKE DECISION.

GREY RECEIPT: DECISION ENVIRONMENT STABILISED.

GOLD RECEIPT: ANNOUNCEMENT SUCCESSFULLY ANNOUNCED.

Communications wanted more gold receipts.

Legal wanted fewer colours.

Treasury wanted receipts that expired before payment was due.

The Prime Minister rehearsed the launch line.

"Every decision will now come with proof."

Legal objected to every.

Policy objected to decision.

Treasury objected to proof, because proof often became an invoice.

The revised line became: "Major areas of government activity will be supported by clearer records of responsible progress where decisions are appropriately capable of confirmation."

Everyone agreed it sounded transparent because it was impossible to see through.

## ACT THREE

At midday, the first National Decision Receipt was printed.

It emerged from a borrowed office printer with a narrow strip of toner across the middle and the words CONFIRMATION PENDING printed twice.

Communications said the duplication showed momentum.

Digital said the printer had interpreted the policy correctly.

Legal said pending confirmation was safer than confirmed confirmation.

Treasury asked whether toner counted as delivery.

The Cabinet Secretary read the receipt aloud.

REFERENCE: NDR-000001.

SUBJECT: NATIONAL DECISION RECEIPT.

ACTION: RECEIPT ISSUED.

OUTCOME: RECEIPT AVAILABLE.

NEXT STEP: RECEIPT REVIEW.

The Prime Minister said this proved the system worked.

The junior adviser said the first receipt appeared to be a receipt for making receipts.

Policy said pilots often began with themselves.

Communications said self-referential delivery was still delivery if the branding was clear.

Legal said the receipt should not be described as evidence of anything beyond its own existence.

Treasury said existence costs should be contained.

Digital refreshed the portal and accidentally generated 4,000 duplicate receipts for the same meeting.

The Cabinet Secretary said this demonstrated scale.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon press conference began with the Prime Minister beside a lectern marked DECISIONS YOU CAN KEEP.

"Today, we are giving the public a clear record of responsible government action," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked what decision had been made.

Communications signalled "framework".

Policy signalled "phase one".

Legal signalled "do not identify the noun".

The Prime Minister said the decision was to ensure decisions were more clearly recognised as decisions once they had reached the proper point of decision recognition.

Another reporter asked whether any services would improve because of the new system.

Treasury said improvement should be assessed against baseline receipt availability.

Digital said users would be able to search by department, date, confidence level, and whether the decision had led to any known consequences.

Legal asked for the consequences filter to be disabled.

Communications said the portal would let citizens see that government was not just talking, but also documenting the talking in a more accountable format.

The reporter asked whether the government was mistaking paperwork for progress.

The Cabinet Secretary replied that paperwork was how progress introduced itself to institutions.

The junior adviser said the government had invented a receipt to prove it had bought time.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Decision Receipt would restore trust, increase clarity, and ensure every serious act of government left behind a responsible, printable trace.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested guidance on missing receipts.

Digital said no receipt could be considered missing unless there was a receipt confirming its absence.

Treasury asked whether absence could be cheaper in bulk.

Communications drafted a line saying the system had already generated unprecedented levels of confirmable activity.

**END**
