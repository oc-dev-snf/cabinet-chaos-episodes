# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 211
**Title:** The National Action Cushion
**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister arrives to find every chair in the briefing room fitted with a bright blue cushion.

"Why has Cabinet become a budget airline lounge?"

The Permanent Secretary smooths a corner of fabric.

"These are National Action Cushions."

"They are cushions."

"They are cushions with a mandate."

The comms director explains that the public respond well to visible comfort during periods of national effort.

"Comfort is not effort."

"No," says the comms director. "But it helps effort look less pointy."

Each cushion has a small embroidered slogan:

**SUPPORTING DELIVERY.**

The Minister sits down and immediately sinks three inches below the table.

"I can no longer see the agenda."

"That is an accessibility issue," says the policy lead.

"It is a furniture issue."

The junior official notes that the cushions were procured under the Rapid Practical Reassurance Framework.

The Minister stares at her.

"There is a framework for soft things?"

"Only since Tuesday."

## ACT ONE

The cushions were originally ordered for a stakeholder roundtable where the chairs were described as "hostile to consensus."

By lunchtime, they had become the centrepiece of a cross-government plan to soften the experience of hard decisions.

The Permanent Secretary presents the first slide.

"The cushion creates a managed pause between intention and discomfort."

"That is called sitting down."

"In unmanaged settings, yes."

The comms director wants the cushions visible in broadcast shots.

"They imply support."

"They imply nobody checked the chair height."

The policy lead says there will be four approved cushion positions.

Forward-facing means readiness.

Sideways means listening.

Stacked means resilience.

Absent means decisive restraint.

The Minister asks whether a missing cushion can be a message.

"Everything can be a message if the line is agreed first."

Legal asks whether the word action creates an obligation.

The Permanent Secretary proposes renaming them Delivery Support Interfaces.

The Minister says that sounds like a sofa applying for a grant.

The name is parked, which everyone knows means it will return with a badge.

## ACT TWO

Departments begin submitting cushion requirements.

Transport wants wipe-clean covers for difficult timetables.

Health asks for lumbar support but says this should not be interpreted as structural criticism.

Education requests smaller cushions to avoid raising expectations.

The Treasury asks whether existing coats can be folded and counted as legacy support.

The junior official circulates the Cushion Deployment Grid.

There is a column for urgency, a column for optics, and a column marked "squash risk."

The Minister points at it.

"Is squash risk political or physical?"

"Both, if the chair is shared."

The comms director receives a message from the lobby team.

A photograph has leaked of a senior official using three cushions while announcing a programme of restraint.

The headline is immediate:

**GOVERNMENT FINDS EXTRA PADDING.**

The room goes silent.

The Permanent Secretary says the key is to distinguish between comfort and excess comfort.

"How many cushions is excess comfort?"

"That depends on the narrative weight of the chair."

The Minister removes his cushion and places it on the table.

"This meeting is now on a firmer footing."

The cushion slowly slides into his water glass.

## ACT THREE

An emergency Soft Furnishings Response Cell is convened.

Facilities says the covers are machine washable but not media washable.

The comms director proposes a demonstration of disciplined cushioning.

"We show the Minister sitting on one cushion, briefly, with purpose."

"I am not doing a controlled sit."

"It polls better than standing near a problem."

The policy lead suggests issuing guidance that cushions are not benefits, perks, or metaphors.

The Permanent Secretary objects.

"They are definitely metaphors."

"For what?"

"For the state's continuing commitment to absorbing impact."

The Minister says the state should try preventing some impact before upholstering it.

The junior official, who has been measuring chair heights with a ruler, says the cushions make the front row look taller than the back row.

The comms director smiles.

"That could imply momentum."

"It implies the back row cannot see."

The Minister orders all cushions removed from the announcement room.

The Permanent Secretary asks whether that creates a visual support gap.

"Good," says the Minister. "Let the gap have a go."

## CLIMAX

The afternoon announcement is moved to a room with plain chairs, plain walls, and absolutely no approved fabric.

The comms director looks unsettled.

"It feels exposed."

"That is because nobody has dressed the delay as upholstery."

The first reporter asks whether the government spent the morning debating cushions.

The Minister nods.

"Briefly."

The Permanent Secretary inhales.

"And usefully."

The Minister continues.

"We have decided that support is not something you can embroider onto a square and place beneath a problem."

A second reporter asks whether the National Action Cushion programme is cancelled.

"It is being reclassified."

The comms director brightens.

"As what?"

"As cushions."

There is a pause.

The Minister adds, "Where a chair is uncomfortable, we will fix the chair. Where a policy is uncomfortable, we will fix the policy. Where a meeting is uncomfortable, we will try having fewer of them."

The junior official writes this down, then underlines "try."

## TAG

Back in the department, the cushions have been stacked in a cupboard marked TEMPORARY.

The Permanent Secretary says they may still be useful for difficult visitors.

The Minister says difficult visitors can have tea.

The comms director enters holding one final cushion.

It has been embroidered incorrectly.

Instead of **SUPPORTING DELIVERY**, it says:

**DELIVERING SUPPORTING.**

Everyone looks at it.

The Minister sighs.

"Fine. Put that one in policy."

**END**
