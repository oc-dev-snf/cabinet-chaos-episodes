# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 178
**Title:** The National Context Lamp
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office switched on the National Context Lamp.

It was a desk lamp with a brass pull chain, positioned beside a stack of unread briefings and labelled CONTEXT in removable vinyl.

The Cabinet Secretary said the lamp would illuminate decisions without forcing anyone to look directly at them.

Communications admired the warmth.

Policy admired the ambiguity.

Treasury asked whether the bulb was recurrent spending.

Legal asked whether illumination created knowledge, implied knowledge, or merely reduced the department's ability to deny the shape of nearby objects.

Digital offered to build a dashboard showing which rooms had been contextually lit.

The junior adviser asked why a government needed a lamp to understand its own policy.

The Cabinet Secretary pulled the chain.

The lamp flickered twice and came on.

Everyone agreed the room looked more informed.

Nobody read the briefing.

## ACT ONE

By 08:15, departments were queueing to stand under the lamp.

Transport brought a timetable with the dates folded underneath.

Health brought a recovery plan with the word pathway highlighted in six colours.

Education brought a consultation response summary and asked whether the lamp made the missing responses more visible.

Treasury said departments could receive context for up to three minutes each, subject to fiscal shading.

Legal drafted a note confirming that context was not advice unless someone had taken notes near it.

Communications proposed calling the lamp "a practical light on complex delivery."

Policy suggested "the situational clarity fixture."

The Cabinet Secretary preferred fixture because it sounded harder to remove.

Facilities reported that the lamp used an old plug adapter from a locked cupboard.

Digital said legacy compatibility was a strength.

The junior adviser said the lamp was pointing at the wall.

Communications said walls were where the public expected things to be projected.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister rehearsed the announcement.

"Today we are bringing context out of the shadows," the Prime Minister read.

Legal asked to replace shadows with areas of reduced visibility.

Treasury asked whether bringing anything out required a removal budget.

Communications wanted a photo of the Prime Minister reaching for the pull chain.

Policy worried that pulling the chain might imply personal responsibility for the light coming on.

Digital suggested a remote switch operated by an anonymised process.

The remote switch turned off the hallway printer.

Facilities said the lamp was already at maximum brightness.

The Cabinet Secretary asked whether maximum brightness sounded too final.

Communications changed it to active glow.

Legal asked whether glow could be challenged.

Treasury said it could be means-tested.

The junior adviser offered to move the lamp closer to the document.

There was a silence.

Policy said proximity could raise expectations.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Context Allocation Panel convened in a room selected for its neutral curtains.

Items with urgent public interest would be placed within the main beam.

Items with unresolved ownership would sit in the softer edge light.

Items requiring cross-government handling would remain in the shade until their handling became less cross.

Communications asked whether the lamp could be angled to make delay look like reflection.

Legal said reflection was acceptable if nobody confused it with a mirror.

Treasury asked whether the bulb could be shared with other initiatives.

Digital opened a spreadsheet named Lamp Use vFINAL2.

The spreadsheet contained one column, titled GLOW STATUS, and seventy-three rows marked pending.

The Cabinet Secretary said this showed demand.

The junior adviser said it showed darkness.

Communications noted that darkness tested well when described as a listening phase.

Facilities warned that the lamp shade was loose.

Policy said loose shade allowed adaptive context.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood beside the National Context Lamp while officials arranged documents just outside its reach.

"This government is shining a practical light on complex delivery," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked which decision had been clarified.

Communications nodded towards the lamp.

The Cabinet Secretary pulled the chain.

The lamp went out.

Legal advised against calling it out, because out implied a previous state of in.

Facilities checked the plug adapter.

Treasury asked whether replacement light counted as emergency procurement.

Digital refreshed the dashboard.

It changed from CONTEXT ACTIVE to CONTEXT TRANSITIONING.

The reporter asked whether transitioning meant nobody knew what was happening.

Policy slid the unread briefing closer to the lamp.

The bulb came back on, very briefly, lighting only the title page.

The title page said SUMMARY TO FOLLOW.

For a moment, the country received context on the absence of context.

The Prime Minister said this proved the system was honest about sequencing.

Communications wrote down honest about sequencing before the light changed its mind.

## TAG

By evening, the National Context Lamp had been moved to a larger room and reclassified as the Situational Illumination Programme.

Treasury requested a bulb-by-bulb value assessment.

Legal prepared guidance on accidental awareness.

Digital launched a public tracker showing whether context was active, transitioning, edge-lit, shade-adjacent, or awaiting an appropriate socket.

Policy proposed a second lamp for delivery.

Facilities said the first lamp now worked only when nobody mentioned outcomes.

The Cabinet Secretary called that a realistic operating condition.

The junior adviser asked whether anyone had finally read the briefing.

Everyone looked at the lamp.

It glowed steadily over the empty space beside the paperwork.

**END**
