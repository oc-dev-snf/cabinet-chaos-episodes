# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 196
**Title:** The National Delivery Shoelace
**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister arrives at the department wearing one polished shoe and one trainer.

Nobody mentions it.

This is how the morning becomes dangerous.

The comms director is already standing beside a display board reading:

**TYING DELIVERY TO RESULTS**

A single enormous shoelace has been taped across a flowchart.

The Minister looks down at his feet, then up at the board.

"Please tell me this is not about my shoe."

"It is about national momentum," says the comms director.

"With laces."

"With visible grip."

The Permanent Secretary hands him a note.

"We have been asked to demonstrate that delivery is joined up."

"So we bought string?"

"Procurement insists it is a performance textile."

The junior official enters carrying a tray of sample aglets.

"These are the reinforced ends."

"Of the policy?"

"Of the lace."

The Minister stares at the board.

"Why is Health tied to Transport?"

"That is the cross-cutting bit," says the policy lead.

"Why is Transport tied to the radiator?"

"That is the bit that has already been announced."

## ACT ONE

The shoelace was introduced by the Delivery Unit after last week's dashboard showed several priorities moving in different directions at once.

The old solution was arrows.

The new solution was tension.

The Minister reads the briefing.

"A single delivery lace will connect departmental objectives, local implementation, fiscal realism, and public confidence."

"It scans well," says comms.

"It sounds like a shoe trying to run the country."

The Permanent Secretary offers the neutral version.

"It is an illustrative device for interdependency."

"Can it be removed?"

"That would imply disconnection."

Finance has added a column estimating savings from reusable knots.

Legal has added a warning that nobody should promise outcomes are tied unless the knots are independently verifiable.

Operations has objected that the lace crosses a fire exit.

The policy lead is optimistic.

"The important thing is that it shows everything is connected."

The junior official points at a loop sagging between Skills and Housing.

"It also shows everything is tripping over Childcare."

There is a pause.

"That," says the Minister, "may be the most accurate graphic we have produced."

## ACT TWO

By lunchtime the National Delivery Shoelace has become a live issue.

A photographer has seen it through the glass wall.

A backbencher has called it "binding".

A think tank has published a note asking whether the government has confused policy coherence with haberdashery.

Comms wants to lean in.

"People understand laces. They are everyday. Practical. No nonsense."

"People also understand falling over," says the Minister.

The Permanent Secretary has convened a Knot Assurance Meeting.

Each knot must be classified as firm, provisional, aspirational, or decorative.

Nobody wants to own decorative.

Finance proposes calling it "non-capital tension".

Legal proposes calling it "not for reliance".

The policy lead proposes calling it "phase two".

The junior official has been sent to map which promises are attached to which promises.

She returns with a hand-drawn diagram that resembles a plate of noodles.

"Good," says comms. "That feels energetic."

"It feels like we dropped government down the stairs," says the Minister.

The Permanent Secretary studies the diagram.

"There is one structural advantage."

"What?"

"If anything fails, it will be difficult to identify the original knot."

## ACT THREE

The afternoon is consumed by the question of whether the shoelace can be launched.

Comms has prepared a clip of the Minister pulling the lace tight while saying, "We are fastening delivery to people's priorities."

The Minister refuses.

"I am not yanking a metaphor on camera."

"It is not a metaphor," says comms. "It is a visual commitment."

"Those are worse. Metaphors can escape."

The Permanent Secretary suggests a lower-risk alternative.

"You could stand near the lace."

"In silence?"

"With intent."

The policy lead has discovered that pulling one end of the lace moves a cardboard hospital target into a cardboard pothole target.

"Is that bad?"

"It depends which announcement is first."

Finance asks whether the lace can be co-funded by departments according to length.

Transport says it should not pay for the section passing through Education.

Education says it has inherited the knot from a previous strategy.

Health says its loop is clinically appropriate.

The junior official quietly unties the radiator.

The entire display collapses half an inch.

Everyone turns.

"Sorry," she says. "It was overheating the timetable."

## CLIMAX

The press room has been rearranged so the display board is just out of shot.

This lasts until a reporter asks why there is a lace trailing from behind the lectern.

The Minister looks at the floor.

One end of the National Delivery Shoelace has wrapped around the lectern wheel.

Comms gives him the look that means say the line.

The Permanent Secretary gives him the look that means do not create a justiciable knot.

The Minister takes a breath.

"Today we were going to show that every priority was tied together. The problem with that is obvious: if you tie everything to everything else, movement in one place can tighten pressure somewhere else. So we are going to do the less photogenic thing. We will name the dependencies, publish who owns them, and stop pretending a knot is the same as a plan."

A reporter asks whether the launch is delayed.

"Yes," says the Minister.

"Because the lace failed?"

"Because the lace worked."

## TAG

Back in the briefing room, the junior official collects the display materials.

The comms director rescues the largest piece of lace.

"We might need this for the resilience announcement."

The Permanent Secretary shakes his head.

"Resilience is elastic."

The Minister finally notices his mismatched shoes.

"Why did nobody tell me?"

Everyone looks at the shoelace in his hand.

"We thought it was part of the strategy," says the policy lead.

**END**
