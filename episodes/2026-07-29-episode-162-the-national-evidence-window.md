# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 162
**Title:** The National Evidence Window
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Evidence Window.

It was described as a practical reform to ensure that decisions would be informed by evidence at the appropriate moment, in the appropriate format, and preferably after the phrase "evidence-led" had already appeared in the morning grid.

The Cabinet Secretary said the window would bring discipline to the timing of facts.

Communications said the public liked evidence, provided it arrived with a headline.

Policy said facts were useful but needed a booking system.

Legal said evidence should not be allowed to wander into live discussions without supervision.

Treasury asked whether existing evidence could be reused from previous announcements.

Digital asked whether the window required a portal.

The junior adviser asked whether evidence normally worked better when it was available before the decision.

The room agreed this was an ambitious interpretation of sequencing.

The proposed system had four daily slots:

08:15: EVIDENCE FOR CONFIDENCE.

10:30: EVIDENCE FOR CONTEXT.

13:00: EVIDENCE REQUIRING LINE CLEARANCE.

16:45: EVIDENCE FOR TOMORROW'S ANNOUNCEMENT.

The Prime Minister said the country deserved decisions that were grounded, rigorous, and not rudely interrupted by late-arriving accuracy.

Everyone agreed this sounded sufficiently modern to brief.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had been asked to submit evidence for the first window.

Transport submitted a chart showing that commuters preferred trains to arrive before apologies.

Health submitted a survey indicating that patients liked services to exist near them.

Education submitted a table proving that smaller class sizes were popular with people who had seen a classroom.

The Home Office submitted a note marked OPERATIONALLY SENSITIVE BUT THEMATICALLY SUPPORTIVE.

Treasury submitted a blank spreadsheet titled Potential Savings From Improved Assumptions.

Communications asked whether any of the evidence had a verb.

Policy said evidence did not need a verb if the minister supplied one.

Legal asked whether the chart could be described as "indicative" until it stopped indicating something awkward.

The Cabinet Secretary introduced the Evidence Window Handling Protocol.

All evidence must be stamped RECEIVED.

Evidence that supports the line may be marked ACTIVE.

Evidence that complicates the line may be marked CONTEXTUAL.

Evidence that contradicts the line must be given time to mature.

The junior adviser asked whether maturing evidence was just evidence in a cupboard.

Treasury said cupboards had lower running costs than inquiries.

Communications said the key thing was to show that evidence had been heard, seen, respected, and kept away from the microphone until it could be helpful.

## ACT TWO

At 10:30, Digital unveiled the Evidence Window Portal.

Each fact received a readiness score, a presentational temperature, and a status colour.

Green meant usable.

Amber meant usable with adjectives.

Red meant requires a wider conversation about methodology.

The train chart turned red after someone noticed the apology line was longer than the punctuality line.

Digital said this showed the portal was sensitive.

Communications asked whether sensitivity could be switched to supportive mode.

Policy suggested reframing the chart as evidence of passenger engagement.

Legal said engagement was safer because it did not imply arrival.

Treasury highlighted the blank spreadsheet, which had turned green because there was nothing in it to dispute.

The Prime Minister entered, holding a draft speech.

"We are putting evidence at the heart of government," the Prime Minister read.

Legal objected to heart.

Treasury objected to putting.

Policy objected to evidence being that close to government without a transition plan.

The revised line became: "We are improving the arrangements by which information can support responsible public choices."

Communications said it sounded like a policy if the room nodded during it.

## ACT THREE

At midday, the evidence began arriving outside its windows.

A regulator published a report early.

A committee released minutes nobody remembered agreeing were finished.

A public body sent a correction to a statistic that had already been corrected into a different statistic.

The Cabinet Secretary called an emergency evidence containment meeting.

Digital proposed a queue.

Policy proposed a queue for the queue.

Treasury asked whether the unscheduled evidence could be counted as future evidence and therefore not today.

Legal said evidence could not be uninvented, but it could sometimes be placed in a careful tense.

Communications drafted a line saying: "The government welcomes the evidence as part of an ongoing evidence environment."

The Prime Minister asked whether the evidence environment was good or bad.

Communications said it depended which sentence came after it.

A minister asked if they could announce a review into the timing of evidence.

The Cabinet Secretary said that would show grip.

The junior adviser said it might also show that the evidence had escaped.

Everyone wrote down "evidence mobility" as a phrase to avoid using in public.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon press conference began beside a lectern displaying the National Evidence Window logo.

"Today, we are making sure decisions are shaped by evidence, not noise," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked whether the government had seen the regulator's report before announcing the policy.

Communications signalled "process".

Policy signalled "broader context".

Legal signalled "do not remember timestamps".

The Prime Minister said the report had entered the evidence environment during a period of active consideration.

The reporter asked whether active consideration meant before or after the decision.

Treasury said time was not the only measure of value.

Digital said the public portal would soon show when evidence was submitted, acknowledged, triaged, cooled, warmed, parked, revisited, and made available for responsible deployment.

Legal asked for "parked" to be renamed "temporarily situated".

Another reporter asked whether the evidence window had been invented to stop evidence getting in the way.

The Cabinet Secretary said evidence was most useful when it arrived through proper channels, at a sustainable pace, with adequate respect for diary pressures.

The junior adviser said the window had not made decisions more evidence-led, but it had given the evidence somewhere to queue.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Evidence Window would protect the public from rushed conclusions, unmanaged facts, and the destabilising effect of knowing things at the wrong time.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested blackout blinds for their evidence windows.

Treasury approved one blind per department, provided it could be described as a transparency accessory.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's grid could say the government had opened the window.

Legal said yes, as long as nobody asked why the room was still stuffy.

**END**
