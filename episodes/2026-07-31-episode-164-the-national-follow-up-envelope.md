# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 164
**Title:** The National Follow-Up Envelope
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Follow-Up Envelope.

It was described as a practical reform to ensure that every public promise would be placed inside a clear, responsible container until somebody was ready to open it.

The Cabinet Secretary said the envelope would bring discipline to the dangerous gap between saying a thing and doing the thing that had been said.

Communications said the public liked follow-up.

Policy said the public liked follow-up less when it contained dates.

Treasury asked whether the envelope could be empty by default.

Legal asked whether empty envelopes created implied contents.

Digital asked whether the envelope needed a QR code.

The junior adviser asked whether the government could simply follow up on things without first creating a national envelope.

The room agreed this was the sort of radical administrative thinking that made minutes difficult.

The envelope would have five classifications:

ACKNOWLEDGED.

UNDER ACTIVE CONSIDERATION.

READY FOR RESPONSIBLE OPENING.

AWAITING CROSS-GOVERNMENT SEAL.

DELIVERED IN PRINCIPLE, PENDING CONTENTS.

The Prime Minister said the country deserved a government that did not leave promises lying around loose on the table.

Everyone agreed this sounded reassuringly stationery-based.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had been asked to submit items suitable for enclosure.

Transport submitted a rail improvement pledge with a note saying the timetable would arrive separately.

Health submitted a community care plan that had already been folded twice to fit inside existing capacity.

Education submitted a standards update printed on paper headed DRAFT FINAL FINAL WITH CONDITIONS.

The Home Office submitted a sealed envelope inside another sealed envelope and asked nobody to develop expectations.

Treasury submitted a savings measure written in pencil so it could remain fiscally agile.

Communications asked whether the envelope could be photographed being carried somewhere purposeful.

Policy suggested a desk shot with a pen, a flag, and no visible address.

Legal said an address might imply a destination.

The Cabinet Secretary introduced the Follow-Up Envelope Handling Protocol.

All promises would be logged, folded, inserted, sealed, recorded as being in motion, and then placed in the Follow-Up Tray.

The Follow-Up Tray was not a backlog.

It was a forward-facing repository of accountable intention.

The junior adviser asked whether anyone had a letter opener.

Treasury said letter openers encouraged unfunded activity.

Communications said the important thing was that the public could see the envelope existed.

## ACT TWO

At 10:15, Digital demonstrated the Envelope Tracker.

Each envelope had a status, a confidence shade, and a small animated flap.

Green meant sealed with confidence.

Amber meant sealed pending line clearance.

Blue meant contents being harmonised.

Grey meant the envelope had entered the furniture estate.

Transport's envelope showed as IN TRANSIT, although nobody could say from where.

Health's envelope showed as OPENED, but only because someone had leaned on it during a capacity meeting.

Education's envelope displayed a warning: CONTENTS EXCEED PRESENTATIONAL LIMIT.

The Home Office envelope refused to render for operational reasons.

Treasury's envelope had no stamp, which Digital described as a deliberate cost signal.

The Prime Minister entered for a rehearsal.

"We are following through on the people's priorities," the Prime Minister read.

Legal objected to through.

Treasury objected to people's.

Policy objected to priorities being singularly attributed before the alignment call.

The revised line became: "We are strengthening the route by which stated intentions can progress toward responsible further handling."

Communications said it had just enough shape to survive a headline.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the envelopes began multiplying.

Every department created sub-envelopes for dependencies, clarifications, ministerial preferences, and phrases that had sounded useful on breakfast radio.

The Follow-Up Tray filled up.

Facilities brought in a second tray.

Legal asked whether a second tray constituted a new programme.

Treasury said it constituted pressure.

Communications proposed calling the trays a delivery landscape.

Policy proposed a Delivery Landscape Board.

Digital proposed a dashboard for the board overseeing the landscape containing the trays holding the envelopes.

The Cabinet Secretary called an emergency sealing meeting.

A minister suggested announcing that the envelope system had already improved accountability because everything was now somewhere.

The junior adviser asked whether knowing that something was somewhere counted as progress.

The Cabinet Secretary said it counted as progress if the previous position was that it might be anywhere.

Everyone paused to appreciate the administrative honesty of this.

Then Communications removed the sentence.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon press conference took place beside a lectern and a large cream envelope marked NATIONAL FOLLOW-UP.

"Today, we are showing that promises will be handled carefully, visibly, and responsibly," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked what was inside the envelope.

Communications signalled "process".

Policy signalled "confidence".

Legal signalled "do not touch the flap".

The Prime Minister said the envelope contained the next stage of the government's commitment to advancing the framework for future delivery.

The reporter asked whether that meant there was a plan.

Treasury said plans were most affordable before they were removed from envelopes.

Digital said the public would soon be able to track every commitment from initial folding to mature sealing.

Another reporter asked whether the envelope was just a way to avoid answering what had happened to yesterday's announcement.

The Cabinet Secretary said yesterday's announcement had been successfully transferred into today's follow-up environment, which demonstrated continuity.

The junior adviser said the system had not delivered the promise, but it had stopped the promise getting coffee on it.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Follow-Up Envelope would replace drift with containment, uncertainty with responsible sealing, and loose ends with a robust national stationery protocol.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested branded envelopes in multiple sizes.

Treasury approved one size, subject to folding.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's grid could say the government was opening a new chapter.

Legal said only if the chapter remained inside the envelope.

**END**
