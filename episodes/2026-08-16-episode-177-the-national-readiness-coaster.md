# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 177
**Title:** The National Readiness Coaster
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office unveiled the National Readiness Coaster.

It was a circular cork coaster on the Cabinet Secretary's desk, printed with the words READY FOR DELIVERY in a font nobody remembered approving.

The Cabinet Secretary said the coaster would provide a stable place to rest major announcements before they became active policy.

Communications liked stable.

Policy liked rest.

Treasury asked whether the coaster counted as infrastructure if enough people put things on it.

Legal asked whether placing a document on the coaster created readiness, implied readiness, or merely protected the furniture from readiness.

Digital offered to create a virtual coaster for remote stakeholders.

The junior adviser asked why readiness needed a coaster.

The Cabinet Secretary placed a briefing note on it.

The briefing note overhung the edge by three centimetres.

Communications said that showed ambition exceeding the platform.

## ACT ONE

By 08:20, departments were submitting items for readiness.

Transport sent a map with several arrows and no dates.

Health sent a plan marked immediate, subject to sequencing.

Education sent three options and asked whether the coaster could hold a fourth.

Treasury said only fiscally neutral items could be placed on the coaster without a moisture assessment.

Legal drafted guidance confirming that the coaster did not guarantee delivery, suitability, durability, availability, or the existence of a cup.

Communications proposed calling it "the visible base of practical action."

Policy suggested "readiness surface."

Everyone preferred readiness surface because it sounded less like something from a conference tote bag.

Facilities warned that the coaster was slightly warped.

Digital said the warp could be captured as a readiness gradient.

The junior adviser said a warped coaster was a strange foundation for national delivery.

The Cabinet Secretary said all foundations had to start somewhere.

## ACT TWO

At 10:05, the Prime Minister rehearsed the launch.

"Today we are putting delivery on a firm footing," the Prime Minister read.

Legal asked to change firm to appropriate.

Treasury asked whether footing implied capital spend.

Communications wanted a close-up of the coaster with a mug beside it to suggest ordinary competence.

Policy objected that the mug might imply something was brewing.

Digital replaced the mug with a QR code.

The QR code opened a page saying READINESS IS BEING PLACED.

The Cabinet Secretary asked whether the page could say ready.

Digital said ready was the final state, and the final state had not completed its consultation with the holding page.

Facilities arrived with four adhesive pads to stop the coaster wobbling.

Legal asked whether adhesive pads constituted a material change to the readiness model.

The junior adviser suggested using a table.

There was a pause.

Communications said a table was too much surface area for the message.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Readiness Surface Steering Group convened around the desk.

Top priority items would sit in the centre.

Items nearing readiness would sit near the rim.

Items with unresolved questions would be held above the coaster until the questions became lighter.

Treasury asked whether the rim could be monetised as a boundary.

Legal said boundaries were useful, provided nobody asked what was outside them.

Communications suggested a photo showing many hands placing one folder on the coaster.

Policy worried that many hands implied complicated ownership.

Digital said the virtual coaster could show one official hand, generated neutrally.

The Cabinet Secretary asked what neutrally meant.

Digital said no rings, no sleeves, no identifiable accountability.

Facilities tested the adhesive pads.

The coaster stopped wobbling and stuck firmly to the desk.

The junior adviser said it was now less mobile than the policy.

The Cabinet Secretary called that maturity.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood beside the desk while the National Readiness Coaster occupied a square of blue cloth usually reserved for signed agreements.

"This government is creating a visible base of practical action," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked what action was ready.

Communications smiled at the coaster.

Policy slid the briefing note towards the centre.

It caught on the adhesive pad and curled upwards.

Legal advised against describing the document as stuck, because stuck implied a failed movement.

Treasury asked whether the adhesive could be shared across departments.

Digital refreshed the QR page.

It changed from READINESS IS BEING PLACED to READINESS IS RESTING.

The reporter asked whether resting meant delayed.

The Cabinet Secretary tried to lift the coaster for the cameras.

The coaster did not move.

For a moment, the country had a perfectly stable readiness surface and nothing ready to put on it.

The junior adviser placed an empty cup on the coaster.

The Prime Minister said this proved the system could support real-world use.

Communications wrote down "real-world use" before anyone could ask what was in the cup.

## TAG

By evening, the National Readiness Coaster had been incorporated into a wider Preparedness Surface Strategy.

Treasury requested a cross-departmental coaster audit.

Legal prepared a disclaimer for heat rings.

Digital launched a public tracker showing whether readiness was placed, resting, centred, rim-adjacent, or awaiting a suitable beverage.

Policy proposed a second coaster for delivery.

Facilities said the first coaster was still stuck to the desk.

The Cabinet Secretary said that demonstrated institutional grip.

The junior adviser asked whether anything had moved.

Everyone looked at the coaster.

It sat there, admirably prepared for whatever might one day be put on top of it.

**END**
