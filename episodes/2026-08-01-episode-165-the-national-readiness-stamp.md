# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 165
**Title:** The National Readiness Stamp
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Readiness Stamp.

It was described as a simple, visible mark that would show the public when a policy was ready to be considered ready.

The Cabinet Secretary said the stamp would end the confusion caused by documents that looked finished merely because they had words in them.

Communications said the public trusted stamps.

Policy said the public trusted stamps more than caveats.

Treasury asked whether readiness could be implied without applying ink.

Legal asked whether the word ready created a duty to begin.

Digital asked whether the stamp could be a status field.

Facilities asked whether anyone had ordered an actual stamp.

The junior adviser asked whether a policy being ready might be demonstrated by doing the policy.

The room agreed this was an interesting long-term ambition.

The stamp would have four approved settings:

READY.

READY SUBJECT TO CONDITIONS.

READY FOR ANNOUNCEMENT, NOT OPERATION.

NOT YET READY, BUT VISUALLY ADVANCED.

The Prime Minister said the country deserved certainty that its government knew when something was nearly in a state to be almost begun.

Everyone wrote that down because it sounded like a line.

## ACT ONE

By 08:20, departments had submitted items for stamping.

Transport submitted a platform improvement plan and asked for the stamp to avoid the timetable pages.

Health submitted a waiting list recovery note marked READY SUBJECT TO PEOPLE.

Education submitted a curriculum update with three competing definitions of the word update.

The Home Office submitted a blank sheet stamped SECURITY SENSITIVE and asked that its readiness be respected.

Treasury submitted a stamp budget consisting of one ink pad, shared nationally.

Communications asked whether the stamp could be photographed descending onto a page in slow motion.

Policy said slow motion implied momentum.

Legal said momentum implied direction.

Digital said direction would need metadata.

The Cabinet Secretary introduced the Readiness Stamp Assurance Process.

Before any document could receive the stamp, it had to pass through the Pre-Readiness Assessment, the Readiness Readiness Check, and the Post-Stamp Risk Reflection.

The junior adviser asked whether this meant the stamp was not ready.

The Cabinet Secretary said the stamp was ready in principle.

Treasury said in principle was cheaper than in practice.

Communications asked whether the ink pad could be described as a delivery accelerator.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, Digital unveiled the Readiness Portal.

Each policy appeared as a tile with a stamp icon, a readiness temperature, and a confidence border.

Green meant ready.

Amber meant ready for further readiness.

Blue meant ready in a controlled environment.

Grey meant the responsible team had gone to find the file.

Transport's tile showed READY, but only after someone filtered out Sundays, strikes, rain, and the word passenger.

Health's tile showed ALMOST READY until Treasury changed almost to fiscally emerging.

Education's tile showed READY SUBJECT TO LEARNING OUTCOMES, which Policy said was either reassuring or circular.

The Home Office tile displayed a padlock and the phrase READINESS CANNOT BE CONFIRMED FOR READINESS REASONS.

The Prime Minister arrived for a rehearsal.

"We are stamping out delay," the Prime Minister read.

Legal objected to stamping out.

Treasury objected to delay being measurable.

Policy objected to delay being blamed before the stakeholder note had blamed complexity.

The revised line became: "We are marking progress through a visible assurance mechanism that supports responsible movement toward delivery."

Communications said it had the rhythm of something nobody could disprove before lunch.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the stamp disappeared.

Facilities said it had last been seen near the Cabinet Room printer.

Digital said a physical object could not disappear from the portal because it had never been uploaded.

Treasury said losing the stamp was a saving if no replacement was authorized.

Legal asked whether any unstamped document could now be deemed unready by omission.

Policy asked whether readiness could be temporarily delegated to a signature.

Communications asked whether the missing stamp created jeopardy or narrative.

The Cabinet Secretary convened an emergency meeting on Stamp Continuity.

Every department was instructed to produce a contingency mark that did not look like the stamp but carried equivalent reassurance.

Transport proposed a tick.

Health proposed a tick with a note.

Education proposed a rubric.

The Home Office proposed a redacted rectangle.

Treasury proposed nothing, on the grounds that absence had a strong record of fiscal discipline.

The junior adviser found the stamp under a folder labelled FINAL HOLDING VERSION.

The room celebrated until Legal confirmed it had already been used on the catering order.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon press conference took place beside a lectern, a fresh ink pad, and a document carefully positioned so no one could read it.

"Today, we are giving the public a clear sign that the government is ready to act responsibly," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked which policy had been stamped.

Communications signalled "all".

Policy signalled "some".

Treasury signalled "none with spend".

Legal signalled "do not press down".

The Prime Minister said the stamp applied to the government's overall direction of travel across a range of priority areas.

The reporter asked whether any actual service would improve because of it.

Digital said users would soon be able to view readiness updates in real time, subject to a short delay while readiness was validated.

Another reporter asked whether the stamp was just a way of making unfinished work look finished.

The Cabinet Secretary said unfinished was an old-fashioned term and that the correct phrase was readiness-sensitive.

The junior adviser said the stamp had made one thing very clear: everyone now knew which documents not to ask about.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Readiness Stamp would replace uncertainty with assurance, hesitation with visible process, and vague intention with a firm rectangular mark.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested personalized stamps.

Treasury approved one, provided every department took turns using the same handle.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's announcement could say the government was leaving its mark.

Legal said only if the mark did not imply contact.

**END**
