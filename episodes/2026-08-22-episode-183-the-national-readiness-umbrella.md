# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 183
**Title:** The National Readiness Umbrella
**Date:** 2026-08-22

## COLD OPEN

The department has received a severe weather warning, a transport update, and a briefing from the centre titled "Preparedness As A Feeling".

No one is sure whether the feeling has been funded.

The Minister arrives holding a bright yellow umbrella with a sticker on the handle.

"Is this ours?"

The Permanent Secretary examines it.

"It appears to be the prototype for the National Readiness Umbrella."

"We have a national umbrella?"

"Not physically. Strategically."

The comms director rushes in, wet from the shoulders down and dry from the neck up, which everyone silently agrees is the department's brand position.

"Good news," she says. "The umbrella photographs well."

"Does it work?"

There is a pause.

"That is a resilience question."

## ACT ONE

The National Readiness Umbrella began as a small public information leaflet about checking local advice during bad weather.

By the time it reached the cross-government grid, it had become a flagship preparedness symbol, an interdepartmental readiness metaphor, and a possible limited-edition item for regional launches.

The Minister wants to know what it actually does.

"It reassures people that government is ready," says the policy lead.

"Ready to do what?"

"To reassure them."

The Permanent Secretary explains that the umbrella is not intended for rain, sun, wind, snow, sleet, uncertainty, operational pressure, legal exposure, or unscripted questions.

"So it is decorative?"

"Minister, it is preventative communications infrastructure."

A junior official points out that the public might prefer practical guidance, like where to find local service updates, emergency contacts, and transport disruption information.

The room receives this with the respectful silence usually reserved for a microphone left on during a private argument.

The comms director unveils the slogan:

**READY TOGETHER, WHATEVER THE WEATHER**

Legal has approved it, provided no one specifies the weather.

## ACT TWO

The first problem is procurement.

The umbrella supplier can deliver four thousand units, but only in clear plastic, which the branding team says makes readiness look "too see-through".

The second supplier can deliver the correct yellow, but the handles are branded with another department's slogan from a cancelled initiative.

The third supplier offers a digital umbrella.

"What is a digital umbrella?" asks the Minister.

"A website that opens when it rains," says the policy lead.

"Does it tell people anything?"

"It asks them to accept cookies."

Meanwhile, the Treasury wants evidence that the umbrella improves readiness, the Cabinet Office wants it aligned with the resilience narrative, and the press office wants to know whether anyone has checked if umbrellas are politically sensitive.

The Permanent Secretary proposes a pilot.

"We distribute three umbrellas across five regions and measure confidence."

"That is less than one umbrella per region."

"Exactly. Scarcity creates engagement."

The junior official again suggests publishing the practical guidance.

This time, the comms director writes it down under "risks".

## ACT THREE

The launch is scheduled outside a community centre because the forecast says "light rain", which the advance team translates as "authentic backdrop".

By morning, the forecast has changed to "weather event with naming potential".

The Minister stands at the lectern under the National Readiness Umbrella, which has been cable-tied open because no one can find the catch.

The press pack gathers under their own umbrellas, all larger and more functional.

The Minister begins.

"Today we are putting readiness in people's hands."

A gust of wind turns the prototype inside out.

The comms director smiles with the force of someone trying to influence physics.

The Permanent Secretary steps forward.

"This demonstrates adaptive openness."

The Minister tries again.

"This government will not leave people exposed."

At that exact moment, rainwater runs off the branding board and into the microphone.

The live stream cuts to a holding slide reading:

**THE NATIONAL READINESS UMBRELLA: COVERING THE ISSUES**

## CLIMAX

Back at the department, the clip has gone everywhere.

One newspaper calls it "a collapse in canopy confidence".

Another runs a diagram explaining how ordinary umbrellas work.

The comms director wants to say the launch showed why readiness matters.

The Permanent Secretary wants an independent review of umbrella resilience.

The Minister wants to know why the public guidance still is not online.

There is a long silence.

The junior official opens a laptop.

"It is online."

Everyone turns.

"I published it during the launch. One page. Local links, emergency contacts, travel updates, what to do before disruption, what to do during disruption."

The comms director refreshes the page.

It loads instantly.

The Permanent Secretary checks the footer.

It has no strategy sub-brand.

The Minister reads for ten seconds.

"This is useful."

The room treats the word as both praise and accusation.

## TAG

At 5:12 PM, the practical guidance becomes the most viewed page on the department's website.

The National Readiness Umbrella is quietly reclassified as "internal learning material".

The Minister asks whether the guidance can be turned into a campaign.

The junior official says it already is one.

"Where is the logo?"

"There isn't one."

The Permanent Secretary looks troubled.

"Then how will people know it is helping them?"

Outside, it stops raining.

In the corner, the umbrella remains upside down, perfectly collecting water.
