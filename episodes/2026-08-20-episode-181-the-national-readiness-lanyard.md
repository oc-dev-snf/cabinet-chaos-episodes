# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 181
**Title:** The National Readiness Lanyard
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office unveiled the National Readiness Lanyard.

It was navy, reversible, and described in the briefing note as a wearable commitment to preparedness.

The Cabinet Secretary said the country needed to see readiness around the neck of government.

Communications said it gave delivery a human loop.

Policy said it created a visible chain between intent and implementation.

Treasury asked whether the chain was literal, metaphorical, or liable to require a separate security assessment.

Legal said a lanyard could imply access, authority, employment, or just having attended a conference.

Digital proposed a QR code linking to a readiness portal.

Facilities asked whether the QR code would survive tea.

The junior adviser asked what the lanyard was for.

The Cabinet Secretary said it was for showing that the person wearing it was ready.

The junior adviser asked ready for what.

Everyone checked the agenda.

The first item was Readiness Definition Readiness.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments were queuing for lanyards.

Transport wanted orange ones so readiness could be seen from platforms.

Health requested wipe-clean versions with a reassuring clip.

Education asked whether pupils could make them as part of a citizenship module, provided nobody called it procurement.

Treasury said each lanyard would need a business case unless it could be classified as morale infrastructure.

Legal circulated advice warning that wearing the lanyard while unready might constitute visual overclaim.

Communications wanted the Prime Minister photographed adjusting one while looking calmly interrupted.

Policy suggested three approved states: ready, nearly ready, and ready to become nearly ready.

Digital's prototype portal asked users to upload a photograph of themselves being prepared.

The Cabinet Secretary said this was excellent because it combined evidence with fabric.

The junior adviser suggested simply telling departments what needed doing.

The room stared at him with the tired concern reserved for people who confuse governance with action.

Communications said telling was risky because it created something that could be misunderstood.

Policy said the lanyard allowed people to understand readiness without the burden of details.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister entered for the cross-government readiness demonstration.

The demonstration involved six officials standing in a line and turning their lanyards from the plain side to the branded side.

"The public want to know that government is prepared," the Prime Minister said.

Communications wrote down prepared and underlined it twice.

Treasury asked whether preparedness had already been announced under a previous heading.

Policy said that was resilience.

Legal said resilience could become preparedness if worn close enough to the chest.

Digital showed the dashboard.

It had a map of the country and a counter reading LANYARD ENGAGEMENT: PENDING.

The Cabinet Secretary asked why the map was grey.

Digital said the lanyards had not yet consented to location sharing.

Facilities reported that several lanyards had been locked in the visitor cupboard because nobody with access had been issued one.

The Prime Minister asked who was currently ready.

Every official looked at their own neck.

Three were wearing old conference passes.

One was wearing headphones.

The Cabinet Secretary announced that mixed neckwear demonstrated the complexity of modern delivery.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Readiness Lanyard Assurance Panel met to decide the launch wording.

Communications proposed: We are putting readiness within reach.

Policy said within reach sounded like it had not been reached.

Treasury preferred: We are putting readiness within existing resources.

Legal objected because existing resources were not a place.

Digital suggested: Scan to see our readiness journey.

The Cabinet Secretary said journeys were good but implied a destination, which could be unhelpful if the destination moved.

Facilities asked whether anyone wanted the box of clips that had arrived separately.

This caused immediate concern.

Legal asked whether the lanyards were valid without clips.

Treasury asked whether clips counted as accessories or enabling works.

Policy said clip readiness should not delay lanyard readiness.

Communications said the absence of clips could be framed as flexibility.

The junior adviser picked up a clip and attached it to a lanyard.

Everyone watched.

Nothing happened.

The Cabinet Secretary said this was precisely why piloting mattered.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00 in the briefing room.

The Prime Minister stood before a row of officials wearing National Readiness Lanyards.

"Today we are making readiness visible, accountable, and close to the heart of public service," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked what the government was ready to do.

Communications looked at Policy.

Policy looked at Legal.

Legal looked at the lanyard.

Treasury looked at the unit cost.

The Cabinet Secretary whispered that the government was ready to proceed with readiness at pace.

The Prime Minister repeated this with a steadier expression than the sentence deserved.

The reporter asked whether any decision had been made.

Digital refreshed the portal.

The counter changed from LANYARD ENGAGEMENT: PENDING to READINESS DISPLAYED.

At that moment, the Prime Minister's lanyard rotated on its own and revealed the reverse side.

VISITOR.

There was a silence long enough for several civil servants to become process.

Communications said the word visitor showed humility.

Policy said it showed government was visiting the concerns of the public.

Treasury asked whether visitors could claim subsistence.

The Prime Minister said responsible leadership meant entering the room, listening carefully, and leaving with the right badge.

## TAG

By evening, the National Readiness Lanyard had been renamed the Visible Preparedness Interface.

Digital added status modes for worn, unworn, scanned, nearly scanned, and lanyard-adjacent.

Legal drafted guidance on accidental authority through neck-based signalling.

Treasury introduced a lanyard cap, with exemptions for strategic loops.

Communications released a photograph cropped just above the word VISITOR.

Policy proposed a readiness roadshow where departments could demonstrate preparedness by arriving already registered.

The Cabinet Secretary said the whole exercise had tightened the connection between government and delivery.

The junior adviser asked whether anyone had found the actual access passes.

Everyone checked their lanyards.

None of them opened the door.
