# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 187
**Title:** The National Readiness Teaspoon
**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The department has discovered that national readiness cannot be stirred into existence with ordinary cutlery.

This is a procurement issue.

The Minister arrives in the morning briefing to find a silver teaspoon on a velvet pad in the middle of the table. Beside it is a laminated card:

**THE NATIONAL READINESS TEASPOON**

"Why is there a teaspoon on a cushion?"

The Permanent Secretary looks pleased in the way only senior officials look pleased when an object has become governance.

"It is not a cushion, Minister. It is a presentation base."

"That has clarified nothing."

The comms director leans in.

"It gives readiness a human scale."

"It's a spoon."

"Exactly. Accessible. Domestic. Stirring."

The policy lead explains that the department has struggled to show the public that it is preparing for difficult scenarios without sounding either alarming or asleep.

The Minister picks up the spoon.

"And this solves that?"

"It demonstrates active preparation."

"For tea?"

"For events."

The junior official says the spoon came from the staff kitchen.

Everyone ignores this because it would make the initiative too affordable.

## ACT ONE

The National Readiness Teaspoon began as a metaphor in a speech draft.

The speech originally said government must "stir readiness into every system".

Legal objected that readiness is not an ingredient.

Finance objected that every system sounded expensive.

Comms objected that stirring sounded calm, which was good, but also slow, which was bad.

The Permanent Secretary therefore convened a working group to decide whether the metaphor should be operationalised, retired, or piloted in two regions with opt-out evaluation.

By 10:00 AM, the metaphor had a launch plan.

"We need an image of practical preparedness," says the comms director.

"People need competent services," says the junior official.

"Yes, but while those are being arranged, people also need an image."

The policy lead has prepared a Readiness Stirring Framework.

It has four phases:

1. Observe the cup.
2. Introduce the spoon.
3. Stir with confidence.
4. Announce increased preparedness.

The Minister reads the list twice.

"Where does the actual preparation happen?"

The Permanent Secretary taps phase three.

"Within the confidence."

## ACT TWO

The spoon rapidly becomes a cross-government coordination problem.

Resilience wants to know whether it can be used in simulated emergencies.

Digital asks whether the spoon needs a dashboard.

Health asks whether it has been risk-assessed for hot beverages.

Transport asks whether stirring implies circular progress.

The Treasury asks whether teaspoons are capital assets if photographed beside a minister.

The comms director proposes a video.

The Minister will stand in a neutral kitchen environment, stir a mug, and say, "Preparedness starts with small actions."

"Will the small action be funding local contingency teams?"

"No, Minister, that is not visually small."

The junior official suggests publishing the list of actual resilience gaps, deadlines, and responsible owners.

The room accepts this as bold, admirable, and unsuitable for filming.

The Permanent Secretary says the teaspoon must avoid seeming partisan, regional, urban, rural, elitist, homespun, alarming, trivial, or insufficiently ambitious.

"So it has to be every spoon and no spoon," says the Minister.

"Precisely."

The policy lead writes this down as the campaign line.

## ACT THREE

The first rehearsal takes place in a borrowed staff kitchenette with lighting equipment, three advisers, and a mug labelled GENERIC PUBLIC CONFIDENCE.

The Minister stands at the counter.

The comms director counts down.

"Three, two, one, readiness."

The Minister stirs.

The spoon bends.

There is a silence normally reserved for failed defence procurement.

The junior official retrieves another spoon from the drawer.

"This one works."

The comms director shakes their head.

"It doesn't have narrative provenance."

"It has structural integrity."

"Yes, but nobody has cleared it."

Meanwhile, the Permanent Secretary has noticed that the mug is empty.

"We cannot show the Minister stirring nothing."

"Why not?" asks the Minister.

Nobody answers quickly enough.

The policy lead suggests adding water.

Legal says water may create an implied promise of flow.

Finance asks whether milk would require agricultural sign-off.

The junior official pours tea into the mug.

Everyone looks at it as if a citizen has entered the room without an appointment.

## CLIMAX

The rehearsal collapses into a practical meeting by accident.

Once the tea exists, people begin making decisions.

The junior official says the local contingency teams still need named contacts.

Transport admits its winter route update is waiting for clearance.

Health says the public guidance has two contradictory phone numbers.

Digital says the dashboard is counting draft actions as delivered actions because draft looks more confident in green.

The Minister puts the spoon down.

"So the teaspoon has revealed that we are less ready than the teaspoon."

The Permanent Secretary tries to intervene.

"It has revealed a temporary alignment opportunity."

"No. It has revealed a list."

The comms director worries that a list is harder to brand than a spoon.

"Good," says the Minister. "A list can be completed."

The junior official begins writing owners and deadlines on the kitchenette whiteboard, which previously contained only a warning about labelled yoghurt.

Within half an hour, the department has resolved three small failures that had survived six readiness summits.

The spoon remains on the counter, unused but nearby.

"Do we still launch it?" asks the policy lead.

The Minister looks at the whiteboard.

"We launch the work."

The comms director winces.

"That is very hard to photograph."

"Then photograph the whiteboard."

## TAG

By evening, the department has published a plain readiness update with responsible teams, dates, and two corrections to previous guidance.

It receives less coverage than the planned spoon video would have done.

It also helps several people.

The Permanent Secretary files the National Readiness Teaspoon under "physical metaphors: paused".

The comms director keeps the velvet pad in case the department needs to dignify a paperclip.

The policy lead updates the framework:

1. Observe the problem.
2. Name the owner.
3. Do the work.
4. Make tea if time permits.

The Minister reads it.

"This is dangerously close to government."

The junior official places the bent spoon beside the kettle.

Someone has taped a label to it:

**NOT FOR SYMBOLIC USE**

By morning, it is missing.

The Permanent Secretary says this proves the metaphor has entered the system.

The junior official says someone probably needed cereal.

Both interpretations are recorded in the lessons learned log.
