# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 169
**Title:** The National Priority Hat
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Priority Hat.

It was described as a simple, visible mechanism for deciding which government priority was currently most important.

Communications said the public wanted leadership they could understand at a glance.

Policy said a hat was one of the few objects in government that could still be put on decisively.

Treasury asked whether the hat was capital expenditure, operational expenditure, or a pre-existing brim.

Legal asked whether wearing the hat created a duty to act on whatever was underneath it.

The Cabinet Secretary said the hat would provide clarity by ensuring every department knew when its issue was, quite literally, on the government's head.

Digital asked whether the hat needed an app.

Facilities said the Cabinet Office had several hats in storage, but one was ceremonial, one was damp, and one had been informally allocated to resilience.

The junior adviser asked whether priorities became more serious when placed in a hat.

The room agreed they became more photographable.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had begun submitting requests to be considered hat-ready.

Transport wanted the hat for infrastructure.

Health wanted the hat for waiting lists.

Education wanted the hat for standards, provided standards could be defined after the photograph.

The Home Office wanted the hat for security, but requested that nobody ask where it was being stored.

Treasury said the hat should remain empty until affordability had completed its own headwear review.

Communications booked a press room with flattering light and a table at precisely the height of seriousness.

Policy drafted the line: "The government is putting priorities where the public can see them."

Legal said that implied the public could also see when priorities were removed.

The Cabinet Secretary proposed a rotation model.

Each priority would receive four minutes in the hat, subject to clearance, presentational weather, and whether another priority had just become louder.

Digital built a dashboard showing current hat status as WORN, PENDING, HELD, RECOVERING, or UNDER BRIM.

The junior adviser suggested deciding the most important issue first.

There was a pause while everyone checked whether deciding was within the terms of reference.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the National Priority Hat entered rehearsal.

The Prime Minister stood before a mirror while Communications tested three expressions: grip, concern, and grip with human concern.

Policy placed a card inside the hat reading DELIVERY.

Treasury removed it and replaced it with VALUE.

Legal added SUBJECT TO PROCESS.

Digital added a QR code.

The Cabinet Secretary said the hat was becoming crowded.

Communications said crowded suggested momentum.

Policy said momentum suggested movement.

Treasury asked whether movement had been costed.

The Prime Minister tried the morning line.

"This government knows its priorities."

The hat display changed to: PRIORITIES BEING KNOWLEDGED.

Communications asked if that sounded too internal.

Policy changed it to: PRIORITIES BEING TAKEN SERIOUSLY.

Legal asked whether seriously could be evidenced.

The Cabinet Secretary said evidence would be provided by the hat remaining visible.

The junior adviser asked what happened when two urgent things needed the hat at once.

Digital said the system supported side-by-side hats in a future release.

Treasury said there would be no future release.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Hat Allocation Meeting began.

Transport arrived with a map and a lanyard for the hat.

Health arrived with a queue chart that had been made less alarming by rotating it slightly.

Education arrived with a glossary of standards-compatible outcomes.

The Home Office arrived with a locked box and said the hat would understand.

Treasury arrived with a smaller hat.

Communications said the smaller hat looked less prime ministerial.

Treasury said it was fiscally credible.

Policy suggested placing the smaller hat inside the larger hat as a symbol of responsible ambition.

Legal warned that nested hats could create nested accountability.

The Cabinet Secretary asked whether the public would accept a hat-based prioritisation framework.

Communications said the public accepted traffic cones, queues, and weather warnings, so there was precedent for objects carrying national meaning.

Digital reported that the online hat tracker had high engagement, mostly from citizens trying to work out whether their local issue was in the brim, the lining, or the unexplained grey zone marked CONTEXT.

The junior adviser said the whole exercise seemed designed to avoid admitting that not everything could be first.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was exactly why the hat was necessary.

## CLIMAX

The press conference began with the National Priority Hat on a stand beside the lectern.

"This government is focused on what matters," the Prime Minister said.

The hat label read: WHAT MATTERS CURRENTLY BEING CONFIRMED.

A reporter asked which priority was in the hat.

Communications signalled confidence.

Policy signalled sequencing.

Treasury signalled restraint.

Legal signalled that naming a priority could prejudice future prioritisation.

The Prime Minister said the hat represented discipline, order, and the ability to concentrate on the issues facing the country.

The hat label changed to: CONCENTRATION IN PROGRESS.

Another reporter asked whether the government had replaced decision-making with a prop.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was an unfair characterisation of a visible governance tool with cross-departmental recognition potential.

Digital announced that citizens would soon be able to subscribe for hat alerts, including "priority added", "priority reviewed", "priority carried forward", and "priority gently removed from public view".

The junior adviser said the hat had finally made the system honest: every department wanted to be on top, nobody wanted to say what came second, and the only thing with a clear position was the furniture.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Priority Hat would restore confidence by showing that priorities were not drifting, but being held in a structured place pending the right moment to wear them.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested their own hats.

Treasury approved a shared brim.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's message could be "government has its thinking cap on."

Legal said only if the cap was not expected to think.

**END**
