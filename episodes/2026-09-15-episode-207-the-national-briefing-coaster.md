# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 207
**Title:** The National Briefing Coaster
**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister arrives to find every water glass in the Cabinet room sitting on a square cardboard coaster.

Each coaster carries the same sentence in a calm government typeface:

**CONFIDENCE REQUIRES A STABLE BASE.**

"Why has my drink been given a slogan?"

The Permanent Secretary does not look up from the seating plan.

"It is the National Briefing Coaster."

"It is a beermat."

"No, Minister. A beermat implies leisure. This is a compact confidence platform."

The comms director taps one with a pen.

"It solves three problems at once: condensation, message discipline, and visible grip."

"Visible grip on what?"

"The glass, initially."

The policy lead hands over a draft.

"The coaster demonstrates that government can put support underneath complex pressures."

The Minister lifts his glass.

The coaster sticks to the bottom, travels three inches, and falls into his lap.

The junior official writes something down.

"Please tell me you did not just minute that."

"I minuted it as a live resilience stress test."

## ACT ONE

The coaster began as a low-cost way to make a press briefing table look less damp.

By half past eight, it had become a cross-government standard for stabilising difficult announcements.

The Minister reads the submission.

"Departments will be encouraged to place the coaster beneath all priority beverages during high-scrutiny activity."

"Why are beverages now part of delivery?"

"They are where people look when avoiding questions."

The comms director has ordered six hundred coasters with slightly different messages.

One says **DELIVERY HAS TRACTION**.

One says **WE ARE UNDERPINNING CHANGE**.

One says **THIS SURFACE IS LISTENING**.

Legal objects to the last one.

The Permanent Secretary proposes a Coaster Assurance Board.

"To assure the coasters?"

"To assure the assurance around the coasters."

The junior official says Facilities wants to know whether old coasters can still be used.

"What do old coasters say?"

"Some say Best Local Pub Quiz 2024."

The room goes quiet.

The comms director says that could accidentally imply institutional memory.

## ACT TWO

Departments begin competing for coaster language.

Health wants washable confidence.

Transport asks whether a coaster can be delayed without being late.

Education proposes a phonics-friendly version: **MAT. CUP. PLAN.**

The Treasury asks whether departments can save money by putting cups directly on the table and calling the table a legacy coaster.

The policy lead unveils the Coaster Maturity Model.

Stage one is absorbency.

Stage two is alignment.

Stage three is circular stakeholder engagement, despite the coaster being square.

Stage four is full surface integration.

The Minister points to stage four.

"That is just a table."

"A table with embedded lessons."

"From what?"

"Earlier dampness."

The comms director receives a message from the press office.

A reporter has obtained a photograph of a ministerial mug sitting half on, half off, the coaster.

The headline is already forming.

The Permanent Secretary says the image must be treated as a proportionate misalignment event.

The Minister says it is a cup.

"Precisely," says the Permanent Secretary. "The public understands cups."

## ACT THREE

An emergency meeting is convened to decide whether the launch should still proceed.

The launch is for the coaster.

The emergency meeting has no coasters, because all coasters have been quarantined for message consistency.

The Minister watches a ring of condensation form under his glass.

"This is absurd."

"It is unfortunate optics," says the comms director.

"It is water on wood."

"That is one possible stakeholder reading."

The policy lead proposes a rapid relaunch as the National Surface Respect Programme.

The Permanent Secretary says relaunching before launching may confuse the timeline.

The junior official suggests wiping the table.

Everyone turns to look at her.

"With what?"

"A cloth."

The Permanent Secretary pauses.

"Do we have cloth policy cover?"

Facilities confirms they have cloths, but not cloth messaging.

The Minister takes the cloth and wipes the table himself.

For one unsettling second, the room contains a practical solution.

## CLIMAX

The press briefing begins with a row of glasses, each perfectly centred on a National Briefing Coaster.

The Minister stands behind the lectern.

A reporter asks whether the coaster initiative is a serious policy or a symbolic one.

The comms director holds up two fingers from the side of the room, meaning "both".

The Minister looks down at the coaster beneath his glass.

"It is a reminder," he says, "that support is useful when it supports something."

The Permanent Secretary closes his eyes.

"It is also a reminder that a coaster works best when no one has to announce it."

The room goes still.

The reporter asks whether that means the programme is cancelled.

"No," says the Minister. "It means the programme has successfully returned to being stationery."

The junior official slides him a note.

It reads: **Technically catering supplies.**

He folds it into a smaller square and places his glass on it.

## TAG

Back in the Cabinet room, the branded coasters have been collected in a sealed evidence envelope.

The Permanent Secretary says they may be needed for a lessons-learned display.

The Minister points to the bare table.

"And the glasses?"

"Stable, Minister."

"Without a national programme?"

The junior official enters carrying a tray.

"Facilities says the cloth has been requested by three departments."

The Minister sighs.

"For cleaning?"

"For a pilot."

**END**
