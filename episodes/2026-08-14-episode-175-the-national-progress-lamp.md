# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 175
**Title:** The National Progress Lamp
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office introduced the National Progress Lamp.

It was a desk lamp with three settings: on, off, and under review.

The Cabinet Secretary said the lamp would give the public a visible signal that work was happening, without trapping ministers in the narrow language of completion.

Policy liked visible.

Communications liked signal.

Treasury asked whether the bulb had been procured through an existing illumination framework.

Legal asked whether the lamp being on constituted a formal commitment to brightness.

Digital offered to connect it to the dashboard, then admitted the dashboard was still connected to a test version of last quarter.

The junior adviser asked what the lamp actually measured.

The Cabinet Secretary switched it on.

The room became slightly brighter.

Communications said that was the strongest progress indicator they had seen all week.

## ACT ONE

By 08:25, departments were competing for lamp time.

Transport wanted it pointed at a map.

Health wanted it pointed away from a waiting list.

Education asked whether progress could be shown through a softer bulb.

The Treasury insisted that any use above forty watts required a savings narrative.

Legal produced guidance confirming that "the lamp is on" should not be interpreted as "the issue is resolved."

Communications drafted a line saying the government was "shining a practical light on delivery."

Policy worried shining sounded too active.

The Cabinet Secretary suggested "placing an appropriate level of illumination near delivery."

Everyone approved this because it made less sense, and therefore contained fewer promises.

Facilities entered with an extension lead and asked who had booked the socket.

Nobody had booked the socket.

The junior adviser said progress depending on an unbooked socket felt thematically dangerous.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was precisely why the lamp was needed.

## ACT TWO

At 10:10, the Prime Minister rehearsed the launch.

"Today we are turning on progress," the Prime Minister read.

Legal asked to replace turning on with supporting the conditions for.

Treasury asked whether progress should be capitalised if it now involved equipment.

Communications wanted the lamp in shot, but not so close that reporters could ask why it was flickering.

Digital said the flicker was temporary while the system updated.

Facilities said the flicker was because someone had plugged in a kettle.

Policy proposed calling it dynamic responsiveness.

The Cabinet Secretary asked whether the kettle was policy-relevant.

Treasury said it depended who paid for it.

The junior adviser suggested moving the lamp to a socket that worked.

There was a long silence.

Communications said moving the lamp might imply the original position had failed.

Legal said leaving the lamp where it was might imply the original position was deliberately dim.

The Prime Minister asked if there was a third option.

Facilities handed over a battery pack labelled CONFERENCE USE ONLY.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Progress Lamp Assurance Group met to decide what each brightness level meant.

Full beam meant action was under way.

Half beam meant action was being scoped.

No beam meant action was being considered in a different room.

The Cabinet Secretary asked what under review meant.

Legal said under review meant the lamp was neither confirming nor denying the existence of light.

Communications suggested amber.

Digital said the lamp did not have amber.

Policy suggested calling warm white amber-adjacent.

Treasury asked whether amber-adjacent could be delivered within the existing colour envelope.

Facilities warned that the battery pack had twenty-two minutes left.

The Cabinet Secretary asked whether twenty-two minutes was enough for a national progress announcement.

Communications said it was more than enough, provided nobody requested progress.

The junior adviser asked if the public might find a lamp a bit thin.

Policy said thin was acceptable if it was cross-government.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood behind the lectern as the National Progress Lamp glowed beside a folder marked DELIVERY EVIDENCE.

"This government is placing an appropriate level of illumination near delivery," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked what had actually been delivered.

The lamp flickered.

Communications smiled at it as if morale could be transmitted by eye contact.

The Cabinet Secretary whispered that the battery pack was entering reserve mode.

Treasury asked whether reserve mode counted as prudent management.

Legal advised against saying reserve.

Policy suggested transitional brightness.

The reporter asked again.

The lamp went out.

For three seconds, the only visible progress was the red standby light on the microphone.

Digital refreshed the dashboard.

It updated from PROGRESS LAMP ACTIVE to PROGRESS LAMP IN FIELD CONDITIONS.

The junior adviser reached behind the lectern, unplugged the kettle, and plugged the lamp into the wall.

The lamp came back on.

The room applauded before anyone could decide whether applause implied delivery.

## TAG

By evening, the National Progress Lamp had been folded into a wider Lighting Confidence Programme.

Communications said this showed the government was not wedded to one bulb.

Treasury asked for a dimmer switch with measurable savings.

Legal requested a disclaimer for shadows.

Digital created a public tracker showing whether the lamp was on, nearly on, recently on, or being respectfully prepared for onness.

The Cabinet Secretary said this was a mature evolution of the policy.

The junior adviser asked whether the lamp had solved anything.

Everyone looked at the desk.

The lamp was illuminating the unanswered question beautifully.
