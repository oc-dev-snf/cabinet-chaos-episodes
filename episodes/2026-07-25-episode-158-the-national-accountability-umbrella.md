# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 158
**Title:** The National Accountability Umbrella
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Accountability Umbrella.

It was described as a protective framework under which responsibility could be shared without anyone becoming unnecessarily damp.

Communications said the public wanted accountability.

Policy said accountability required ownership.

Legal said ownership required risk.

Treasury said risk required a reserve, preferably someone else's.

The Cabinet Secretary said the umbrella would provide visible cover while departments worked through the practical implications of standing under it.

The junior adviser asked whether accountability meant saying who was responsible.

Communications said the word who was often unhelpfully narrow.

Policy suggested "system-wide stewardship".

Legal preferred "collective situational custody".

Treasury asked whether custody came with staffing costs.

Digital offered to build an Umbrella Portal showing which departments were covered, partially covered, or waiting for weather.

The Prime Minister said the country deserved a government that could face the storm with a strong framework, a clear message, and no avoidable splashback.

Everyone agreed this sounded serious, provided nobody opened the umbrella indoors.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had been asked to nominate issues suitable for accountable coverage.

Transport submitted a delayed project and asked whether delays counted as weather.

Health submitted a pathway review with responsibility distributed across seven diagrams.

Education submitted a note explaining that lessons would be learned once the learning environment had been assessed.

The Home Office submitted a black folder labelled "weather-sensitive".

Treasury submitted a spreadsheet proving that accountability became more affordable when described as resilience.

Communications rejected several entries because they used the word failure.

Policy said failure was sometimes the thing accountability was about.

Communications said that was exactly the reputational problem.

The Cabinet Secretary proposed a standard umbrella pack:

ONE ISSUE REQUIRING COVER.

ONE MINISTERIAL LINE WITH HANDLE.

THREE SUPPORTING RIBS OF CONTEXT.

NO DIRECT DRIPS.

Legal asked whether a drip could be defamatory.

Policy said only if it landed on a named decision-maker.

The junior adviser asked what would happen if the umbrella blew inside out.

The Cabinet Secretary said that would be treated as a transparency event.

Treasury asked whether transparency events could be scheduled after the quarter closed.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister rehearsed the launch statement.

"This government takes full responsibility."

The room became extremely still.

Legal objected to full.

Treasury objected to responsibility.

Communications objected to takes, because it sounded like someone might later ask where it had been taken.

The revised line became: "This government recognises the importance of responsibility being appropriately located within a wider delivery climate."

Digital displayed the Umbrella Portal:

COVERAGE STATUS: EXTENDING.

RESPONSIBILITY MOISTURE: MANAGED.

MINISTERIAL DRIP RISK: MODERATE.

PUBLIC CONFIDENCE: WEATHER DEPENDENT.

Communications liked weather dependent because it sounded honest without admitting anything.

Policy said the public might ask what the weather was.

Legal said nobody should speculate on meteorological liability.

Meanwhile, one department accidentally published a sentence saying it was responsible for a decision.

Communications asked whether the sentence could be amended to "involved in the decision environment".

Legal asked whether the sentence had already been screenshotted.

Treasury asked whether involvement was cheaper than responsibility.

The Cabinet Secretary ordered the sentence moved under the umbrella until it stopped looking dry.

## ACT THREE

At midday, the Accountability Umbrella Board met in the Cabinet Room.

An actual umbrella had been placed at the centre of the table for symbolism.

Communications said it photographed well.

Policy said it created a strong sense of shared shelter.

Legal said nobody should touch it without a handling note.

Treasury asked whether it had been procured centrally.

The Prime Minister asked who would stand under it first.

The Cabinet Secretary explained that the purpose of the umbrella was not to assign shelter, but to demonstrate the government's readiness to consider shelter as a collective principle.

Digital updated the portal:

UMBRELLA POSITION: CENTRAL.

COVERAGE MODEL: COLLABORATIVE.

ACCOUNTABILITY FLOW: DIFFUSED.

HANDLE OWNERSHIP: TO BE CONFIRMED.

The junior adviser said an umbrella without someone holding it was just a small roof with ambitions.

Communications said that was not the tone.

Policy said it might be the architecture.

Treasury said roofs were capital expenditure.

Legal said ambitions should be clearly separated from commitments.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon press conference began with the Prime Minister beside a lectern marked ACCOUNTABILITY WITH COVER.

"Today, we are putting accountability where the public can see it," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked where exactly that was.

Communications signalled "framework".

Policy signalled "shared".

Legal signalled "do not point".

The Prime Minister said: "It sits across government as part of a broad, responsible umbrella of delivery."

Another reporter asked who was responsible for the problem the umbrella was meant to cover.

The Cabinet Secretary stepped forward and explained that modern accountability was not a person but a managed relationship between evidence, context, lessons, and forward momentum.

Digital refreshed the portal.

It displayed:

RESPONSIBLE PARTY: SYSTEM.

LESSONS STATUS: AVAILABLE FOR LEARNING.

COVERAGE CONFIDENCE: BROAD.

DRIP VISIBILITY: UNDER REVIEW.

The reporter asked whether this meant nobody was accountable.

Legal said nobody had said nobody.

Communications said accountability had never been more visible.

Policy said visibility was the first stage of grip.

Treasury said grip should be costed before anyone held anything.

The junior adviser said the government had invented an umbrella so large that everyone could stand under it and still claim they were somewhere else.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Accountability Umbrella proved the government was prepared to take responsibility seriously, collectively, and in weather-appropriate ways.

## TAG

By evening, departments were asked to submit umbrella usage returns.

Most replied that they had not used the umbrella, but had benefited from the shade.
