# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 190
**Title:** The National Announcement Thermostat
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The department has discovered temperature.

Not actual temperature, which remains the responsibility of facilities, estates, three procurement frameworks, and one window nobody is allowed to open because it is listed as heritage airflow.

Political temperature.

A small white thermostat has been fixed to the wall outside the Cabinet room. It has no wiring, no batteries, and a bright red sticker reading:

**SET NATIONAL MOOD BEFORE ANNOUNCING**

The Minister stops in the corridor.

"Why is there a thermostat beside the crisis board?"

The Permanent Secretary gives the serene nod of someone introducing a metaphor with a budget code.

"It is the National Announcement Thermostat."

"Does it control anything?"

"Tone."

The comms director taps the plastic casing.

"At the moment, the public mood is reading lukewarm with localised draughts."

"That is a weather forecast for patience."

"Exactly, Minister. We need to announce at room temperature."

The junior official peers at the dial.

"It only has three settings: Reassuring, Robust, and Listening."

The Permanent Secretary smiles.

"Those are the constitutional settings."

## ACT ONE

The National Announcement Thermostat began after a perfectly ordinary departmental update was received as either too urgent, not urgent enough, insufficiently calm, excessively calm, or calm in a way that implied guilt.

The lesson drawn from this was not to write clearer updates.

The lesson drawn was climate control.

The policy lead presents a slide titled Thermal Mood Governance.

"Every announcement has a temperature profile," they say. "If it is too hot, people say we are panicking. If it is too cold, people say we do not care."

"And if it is accurate?" asks the junior official.

The room looks briefly uncomfortable.

"Accuracy is a separate heating zone," says the Permanent Secretary.

The Minister studies the device.

"How do you know the national mood?"

The comms director produces a dashboard showing newspaper adjectives, call-centre wait times, social clips, focus group biscuits consumed, and the number of times the word "pragmatic" has appeared in overnight lines.

"We blend indicators."

"That is not measuring the mood. That is making soup from anxiety."

"A warm soup, ideally."

## ACT TWO

By mid-morning, every team wants access to the thermostat.

Finance asks to set it to Reassuring before publishing cost controls.

Delivery wants Robust for milestones, Listening for delays, and a fourth setting called Nearly There.

Legal asks whether adjusting the dial creates a discoverable record of emotional intent.

Digital asks whether the thermostat needs a user account, two-factor authentication, and a dashboard displaying thermostat engagement.

Facilities objects that a non-functioning thermostat may breach the building's real thermostat policy.

"Can we just call it a gauge?" asks the junior official.

The comms director shakes their head.

"A gauge observes. A thermostat implies leadership."

"It implies heating."

"Leadership often does."

The Minister is handed a draft announcement about a national programme whose latest timetable has been "reprofiled", "reset", "sequenced", and finally "placed into a more deliverable season".

The dial is currently between Robust and Listening.

"What does that mean?"

"Firm empathy," says the policy lead.

"That sounds like being hugged by a filing cabinet."

## ACT THREE

The rehearsal begins with the Minister standing beside the thermostat while the comms director watches a live readout of imagined reception.

"Good morning. Today we are setting out the next phase..."

The comms director raises a hand.

"Too warm."

"I said good morning."

"Yes, but with forward energy."

The Minister tries again.

"This update reflects the work already under way..."

"Too cool."

"It reflects work."

"Precisely. Reflection can read as distance."

The Permanent Secretary suggests adding the phrase "with humility and determination".

The junior official points out that the announcement contains three dates that do not match the implementation note.

Silence settles over the room with excellent insulation.

The Minister turns.

"Why do the dates not match?"

The policy lead checks the papers.

"The implementation note has operational dates. The announcement has confidence dates."

"Those are not dates. Those are wishes wearing numbers."

The comms director glances at the thermostat.

"The mood is cooling."

"Because facts have entered the room."

## CLIMAX

The thermostat is moved into the middle of the table for an emergency alignment session.

Everyone takes turns setting it.

Finance chooses Reassuring.

Delivery chooses Robust.

Legal chooses Listening, but with a written caveat that the device is illustrative.

The comms director chooses a point between all three and calls it Mature Urgency.

The junior official takes the implementation note, the draft announcement, and a pen.

"What if the temperature problem is that the statement says we are on track while the plan says we are changing track?"

The Permanent Secretary looks at the thermostat as if hoping it will intervene.

It does not.

The Minister reads both documents.

"We are saying too much around the thing and not enough about the thing."

"The thing is sensitive," says the comms director.

"So is a kettle. You still have to say whether it is boiling."

The Minister rewrites the opening line:

**The timetable has changed, and here is what changes with it.**

The room waits for disaster.

Nothing happens.

The thermostat remains plastic.

The dashboard refreshes and labels the mood "specific".

"Is specific good?" asks the Permanent Secretary.

"It is new," says the junior official.

## TAG

The announcement goes out at noon.

It is shorter than planned, warmer than a table, cooler than a slogan, and contains the same dates as the implementation note.

The reaction is mixed, which the comms director describes as "a healthy democratic ambient range".

By evening, the thermostat has acquired three handwritten additions:

**CHECK FACTS**

**MATCH DATES**

**DO NOT PRETEND A DIAL IS A PLAN**

The Permanent Secretary commissions a note on whether the additions undermine the integrity of the device.

The Minister leaves them there.

"For once, the room is telling us the temperature."

The junior official opens the forbidden window by two inches.

Nobody notices for ten minutes.

During that time, the meeting improves.
