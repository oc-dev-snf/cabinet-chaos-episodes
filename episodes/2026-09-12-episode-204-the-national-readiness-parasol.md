# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 204
**Title:** The National Readiness Parasol
**Date:** 2026-09-12
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister enters the Cabinet room to find a parasol open over the conference table.

It is indoors.

It is branded.

It says **READY FOR WEATHER** in white letters around the edge.

"Why is the table on holiday?"

The Permanent Secretary looks up from a folder.

"It is the National Readiness Parasol."

"It is a beach object in a crisis room."

"It demonstrates preparedness."

"For sunstroke?"

The comms director adjusts the angle so the slogan faces the door.

"Preparedness is more visible with a canopy."

"So is surrender."

The policy lead points to the metal pole planted in the middle of the table.

"Each department sits beneath its own segment of readiness."

The junior official peers up.

"There are only eight segments."

"Then the smaller departments share shade," says the Permanent Secretary.

The Minister looks at the blocked sightline across the room.

"Excellent. We have invented obstructed accountability."

## ACT ONE

The parasol began as a visual aid for a summer resilience announcement.

By breakfast it had become a cross-government preparedness architecture.

By half past nine it had acquired a risk register, a stakeholder map, and a small bag of sand no one was legally prepared to call ballast.

The Minister reads the briefing.

"The parasol allows readiness to be understood as coverage."

"Coverage from what?"

"Unhelpful exposure," says the comms director.

"That is not policy. That is sunscreen with a press office."

Finance is worried the parasol may imply departments have been underfunded in shade.

Legal is worried that **READY FOR WEATHER** could be taken to include all weather.

Operations is worried the thing cannot fit through the corridor unless it is folded, which comms says may look like retreat.

The Permanent Secretary suggests a controlled opening exercise.

"It is already open," says the Minister.

"Yes, Minister, but not yet formally opened."

The junior official finds a ribbon tied to one rib of the canopy.

"Should I cut it?"

Everyone looks horrified.

"No," says the comms director. "That would imply commencement."

"We are announcing readiness without commencing?"

"We are commencing the readiness to announce."

The Minister stares at the parasol.

"I can feel democracy developing a tan line."

## ACT TWO

Departments submit shade claims by noon.

Transport wants priority coverage for junctions.

Health wants a winter lining added to the summer parasol.

Education asks whether schools should practise opening umbrellas as a civic skill.

The Treasury note says it supports the principle of shade while reserving the right to define sunlight as a local responsibility.

The policy lead unveils the Coverage Confidence Grid.

Green means a department is under the parasol.

Amber means it is near the parasol but experiencing edge effects.

Red means it is publicly visible.

Grey means conditions are still being scoped.

The Minister taps the grid.

"Where is the box for fixing the problem?"

"That is a separate climate of activity," says the Permanent Secretary.

"A climate?"

"A metaphorical one."

"Then why have you brought furniture?"

Comms proposes a launch photo from underneath the canopy.

Legal says photographing from underneath may imply the government is looking up to the problem.

Finance says looking up may create height expectations.

The junior official notices water dripping onto the agenda.

"Is it leaking?"

"No," says the policy lead. "It is stress-testing precipitation."

The Minister watches a drop land on **Item 3: Dry Run**.

"The dry run is wet."

The comms director writes **dynamic realism** in the margin.

## ACT THREE

By early afternoon the parasol has become the meeting.

Nobody can see across the table, so all decisions are passed around the pole as whispered summaries.

The Home Office hears "resilience framework" as "residence paperwork."

Environment hears "coverage gap" as "cabbage app."

The Cabinet Office creates a subgroup to confirm whether the pole is load-bearing in governance terms.

The Minister stands and accidentally lifts one side of the canopy with his head.

Half the room cheers because, for a second, they can see each other.

"There," he says. "Progress."

"Technically that was accidental transparency," says the Permanent Secretary.

"The best kind we have had all week."

A junior press officer enters carrying a phone.

"The broadcasters are asking why the government is launching a weather plan indoors."

The comms director takes the phone as if accepting a difficult sandwich.

"Say indoors is where outdoor resilience is coordinated."

"They also ask whether the parasol works in rain."

Silence.

Everyone looks at the leak.

The junior official reads the label on the handle.

"For decorative use only."

The Permanent Secretary closes his folder.

"That is unfortunate."

The Minister smiles without pleasure.

"No. That is the first accurate line in the whole document."

## CLIMAX

The press conference is moved to the courtyard so the parasol can be seen in its natural habitat.

The wind immediately turns it inside out.

The comms director whispers that this shows flexibility.

Legal whispers that it shows product failure.

Finance whispers that replacement ribs are capital expenditure.

A reporter asks whether the government has confused being covered with being ready.

The Permanent Secretary offers the prepared line.

"The National Readiness Parasol is a visible symbol of our integrated approach to conditions."

The Minister looks at the twisted canopy, the dripping notes, and the officials trying to stand in a shape that suggests confidence.

Then he steps away from the parasol.

"Readiness is not a prop. It is knowing the risk, naming the gap, funding the boring bit, and telling people what happens when the weather arrives. If the plan only works indoors, it is not a plan. It is an ornament with minutes."

The wind takes one branded panel off the parasol and carries it over the courtyard wall.

Nobody moves.

The junior official raises a hand.

"Should we log that as outreach?"

The Minister points at the door.

"Log it as evidence that the weather attended."

## TAG

Back in the Cabinet room, the parasol pole has left a dent in the table.

Facilities places a small cone over it.

The cone says **TEMPORARY READINESS FEATURE**.

The Minister looks at it.

"How temporary?"

The Permanent Secretary checks the works schedule.

"Until the permanent readiness feature is procured."

The junior official writes it down.

"So the hole is fully covered."

The Minister nods.

"And still there."

**END**
