# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 171
**Title:** The National Neutrality Meter
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office unveiled the National Neutrality Meter.

It was a brushed steel device with a small needle, three lights, and a reassuring label reading: MEASURED TONE IN PROGRESS.

Communications said it would ensure every public statement sounded balanced, sober, and impossible to turn into a headline before lunch.

Policy said it would help departments locate the precise point between saying something and saying enough for someone to ask a follow-up.

Legal asked whether the meter measured neutrality, the appearance of neutrality, or the defensibility of having attempted to measure either.

Treasury asked whether the needle could be reused from an existing dial.

Digital said the meter had an online dashboard, but the dashboard itself had not yet passed a neutrality assessment because the word dashboard implied movement.

The Cabinet Secretary explained that modern government required a tool capable of proving restraint without accidentally displaying an opinion.

The junior adviser asked whether people might prefer a clear answer.

The room agreed clear answers were valuable, provided they were presented as future work.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, every department had submitted phrases for calibration.

Transport proposed "journey-focused momentum."

The meter flickered amber.

Health proposed "patient-centred patience."

The needle wobbled, then settled in a zone marked HUMAN BUT NOT COSTED.

Education offered "standards are rising where rising has been identified."

The meter produced a soft click that Facilities described as "possibly disappointment."

The Home Office submitted a blank card in a sealed folder and requested confirmation that silence could be rated firm.

Treasury proposed "responsible support within available realities."

The meter turned green so quickly that Communications asked whether it had been tampered with by fiscal conditions.

Legal introduced a fourth category: NEUTRAL SUBJECT TO CONTEXT.

Digital added it to the dashboard, which immediately became less neutral by having more categories than citizens could reasonably trust.

The Cabinet Secretary announced a Neutrality Meter Handling Protocol.

Nobody would be allowed to speak near the device without first declaring whether their sentence contained optimism, disappointment, urgency, or a number.

The junior adviser asked whether the government was now measuring tone because it had run out of decisions to measure.

Communications said that was exactly the sort of phrase the meter was designed to prevent.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the first ministerial rehearsal began.

The Prime Minister stood behind a lectern while the National Neutrality Meter sat beside the microphone like a very small referee.

Communications handed over the opening line.

"We are listening carefully and acting responsibly."

The meter glowed green.

Policy added "with urgency."

The light shifted amber.

Treasury crossed out urgency and wrote "with appropriate pace."

The light returned to green.

Legal inserted "where appropriate."

The needle moved into a narrow band labelled STATEMENT SURVIVES CONTACT WITH COMMITTEE.

Digital suggested publishing live meter readings during press conferences.

Communications said transparency was useful but only after someone had decided what the transparent thing meant.

The Cabinet Secretary asked for a phrase that sounded active without implying a timetable.

Policy suggested "we are moving this forward."

Treasury asked how far forward.

Legal asked whether forward could be challenged by anyone standing beside the issue.

Communications amended it to "we are maintaining constructive direction."

The meter glowed green and emitted a chime.

The junior adviser said the device had rewarded a sentence that would make nobody wiser.

The Cabinet Secretary said wisdom was not the metric under review.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Interdepartmental Neutrality Board convened to settle a dispute over adjectives.

Transport argued that "ambitious" should be permitted if followed by "phased."

Health said "compassionate" should be allowed in emergencies, winter, and any sentence involving chairs.

Education wanted "world-class" approved as a historical aspiration rather than a present claim.

Treasury opposed "world-class" unless another country was willing to share the invoice.

Legal warned that adjectives created expectations, expectations created records, and records created meetings with folders.

Communications presented a list of words safe for national use: steady, practical, ongoing, balanced, careful, focused, and further.

Policy asked whether "further" required there to have been something before it.

Legal said not necessarily, if handled as a direction of travel rather than evidence of distance.

Digital showed a live public test page where citizens could paste government statements and receive a neutrality score.

The most neutral phrase so far was "details will be set out in due course."

The second most neutral phrase was an empty page with a footer.

The Cabinet Secretary said the tool demonstrated a clear commitment to measured communication.

The meter flashed amber at "clear."

Communications changed it to "observable."

The meter flashed amber again.

Treasury suggested "affordable."

The meter turned green.

The junior adviser asked whether the meter was neutral or simply afraid of cost.

Nobody wrote that down, which gave it a provisional rating of safe.

## CLIMAX

The press conference began with the National Neutrality Meter positioned in full view, though not so full that anyone could read the labels.

"This government is committed to practical, balanced, and carefully paced delivery," the Prime Minister said.

The meter glowed green.

A reporter asked whether that meant a specific policy would happen this year.

The meter went amber before anyone answered.

Communications signalled caution.

Policy signalled context.

Treasury signalled that the year was still under review.

Legal signalled that a year was a defined period and therefore dangerous.

The Prime Minister said the government would proceed in a manner consistent with responsible national expectations.

The meter glowed green.

Another reporter asked whether the device was there to stop ministers saying anything controversial.

The Cabinet Secretary said the device was there to support a high-quality tone environment.

Digital announced that citizens would soon be able to subscribe to alerts when a statement moved from NEUTRAL to NEUTRAL-ADJACENT.

The junior adviser said the meter had solved the central problem of government communication: it made every answer sound fair, calm, and completely unoccupied by information.

The meter hesitated, then turned green.

Communications asked whether that counted as endorsement.

Legal said it counted as a reading.

Treasury asked whether readings could be charged back to departments.

## TAG

By evening, every department had requested its own Neutrality Meter.

Facilities found the original one unplugged.

It was still glowing green.

Legal said that was either impressive, alarming, or beyond the scope of the protocol.

Communications proposed the line: "Even when not connected, the government remains measured."

The meter finally went red.
