# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 161
**Title:** The National Clarity Tray
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Clarity Tray.

It was described as a practical desktop intervention to help government separate urgent matters, important matters, and matters that had become urgent because nobody had wanted to call them important.

The Cabinet Secretary said the tray would restore discipline to the physical handling of national priorities.

Communications said the public liked visible organisation.

Policy said trays were neutral.

Legal said neutrality depended on labelling.

Treasury asked whether existing trays could be reclassified as strategic infrastructure.

Digital asked if the tray required a dashboard.

The junior adviser asked whether the problem was the absence of trays or the absence of clarity.

The room treated this as an early example of material for the bottom tray.

The proposed system had three layers:

TOP: READY FOR ANNOUNCEMENT.

MIDDLE: AWAITING RESPONSIBLE MOMENTUM.

BOTTOM: TOO CLEAR TO CIRCULATE.

Communications immediately objected to the bottom tray.

Legal said the bottom tray was vital because some documents were safer when technically received but socially invisible.

The Prime Minister said the country deserved a government with priorities in order, documents facing upward, and a strong commitment to not losing the paper that explained why nothing had happened.

Everyone agreed this sounded tidy enough to launch.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had been asked to submit items for the prototype clarity tray.

Transport submitted a timetable note marked RAIN-ADJACENT.

Health submitted a delivery plan for reducing the number of meetings about delivery plans.

Education submitted a curriculum update with a sticky note saying ASK WHETHER THIS IS STILL TRUE.

The Home Office submitted a sealed envelope labelled RECEIVED IN PRINCIPLE.

Treasury submitted an empty folder and said it represented future savings.

Communications placed everything in the top tray because the camera angle was better.

Policy moved the empty folder to the middle tray because it had no content but considerable potential.

Legal moved the sealed envelope to the bottom tray because nobody could confirm whether opening it would create knowledge.

The Cabinet Secretary produced guidance on tray etiquette.

All documents must enter from the left.

All hesitations must be logged.

No document may be described as stuck unless it has failed to move after three properly minuted opportunities.

The junior adviser asked whether this meant government was now measuring progress by stationery migration.

Treasury said migration was a loaded term and stationery had no fiscal nationality.

Communications said the important thing was that the tray looked purposeful from a distance.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, Digital unveiled the Clarity Tray Tracker.

Each document received a movement score, a confidence colour, and a projected seriousness rating.

The first item, the timetable note, moved from middle to top after a senior official nudged the desk.

Digital recorded this as system responsiveness.

Legal asked whether accidental movement constituted intent.

Policy said intent could be inferred from direction.

Treasury said direction was cheaper than delivery.

Communications drafted a post saying: "Government priorities are moving."

The Cabinet Secretary added a new tray category called TRANSITIONALLY ACTIVE.

Nobody knew whether it went above or below the middle tray, so Digital created a virtual shelf.

The shelf immediately filled with items that had previously been described as almost ready, broadly agreed, operationally live, or awaiting final ministerial patience.

The Prime Minister rehearsed the launch line.

"We are bringing clarity to the heart of government."

Legal objected to heart.

Policy objected to clarity.

Treasury objected to bringing, because it implied transportation costs.

The revised line became: "We are supporting clearer handling arrangements for material connected to national activity."

Communications said it had the rhythm of reform if read quickly.

## ACT THREE

At midday, the National Clarity Tray jammed.

The top tray could not be lifted because Communications had filled it with announcements that were ready in tone but incomplete in substance.

The middle tray sagged under the weight of responsible momentum.

The bottom tray had become too full to deny knowledge of.

The Cabinet Secretary called an emergency desk resilience meeting.

Digital suggested scanning all documents and creating a paperless tray.

Legal said a paperless tray might still count as a tray if it retained the anxiety of one.

Treasury asked whether a virtual jam was cheaper than a physical jam.

Policy proposed a tray reset.

Communications said reset sounded like failure.

The Prime Minister suggested refresh.

Everyone relaxed.

A minister entered with a new priority and asked where it should go.

The room stared at the tray.

The Cabinet Secretary said new priorities must first be assessed against existing priorities, pending a review of whether existing priorities had priority over the prioritisation process.

The junior adviser quietly placed the new priority on the windowsill.

Digital logged it as an external holding environment.

## CLIMAX

The afternoon press conference began with the Prime Minister standing beside a polished desk and three visibly labelled trays.

"Today, we are making government clearer, quicker, and better organised," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked which policy had become clearer.

Communications signalled "system".

Policy signalled "handling".

Legal signalled "avoid the tray contents".

The Prime Minister said clarity was not only about knowing what had been decided, but also knowing where the papers were while the decision matured.

Another reporter asked whether any of the documents in the top tray had actually been implemented.

Treasury said implementation was only one form of value.

Digital said the public dashboard would show when an item entered, moved, paused, rotated, returned, or gained contextual altitude.

Legal asked for "returned" to be renamed "repositioned".

The reporter asked whether the government had confused administration with action.

The Cabinet Secretary said administration was the route action took when it wanted to be properly witnessed.

The junior adviser said the tray had not created clarity, but it had given confusion somewhere official to sit.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Clarity Tray would ensure priorities were visible, accountable, and stacked according to the seriousness of their current handling status.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested additional trays for overflow clarity.

Treasury approved one tray per department, provided it could also be used to store rejected spending bids.

Communications asked whether the bottom tray could be renamed the Future Top Tray.

Legal said yes, as long as nobody took that literally.

**END**
