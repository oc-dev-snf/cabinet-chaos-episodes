# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 173
**Title:** The National Urgency Shelf
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office announced the creation of the National Urgency Shelf.

It was a plain grey shelf in Meeting Room 4B, labelled ITEMS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE ATTENTION in laminated Helvetica.

The Cabinet Secretary explained that government had too many urgent issues and not enough places to put them while deciding how urgent they were.

Policy said the shelf would provide structure.

Treasury asked whether structure required maintenance.

Legal asked whether placing an issue on the shelf constituted acceptance of the issue.

Communications said the key message was that urgent matters would now be visibly close to action.

The junior adviser asked whether "close to action" meant action.

Communications said it meant adjacent to action, which was more flexible.

By 07:12, the shelf held a red folder, three briefing notes, a printout of an email marked PLEASE ADVISE, and a sandwich someone had labelled "time-sensitive."

Facilities said the shelf had exceeded its rated load.

The Cabinet Secretary said that proved demand.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments were competing for shelf space.

Transport wanted a place for decisions that were ready to move but not yet authorised to travel.

Health requested half a shelf for matters that had become urgent while awaiting clarification on whether they had previously been urgent.

Education asked whether an item could be put on the shelf as a learning opportunity.

Treasury proposed a charging model where urgency could be rented by the hour.

Legal warned that paid urgency might imply unpaid matters were not urgent, which could be difficult if they were.

Communications suggested a traffic-light system.

Policy said traffic lights had already created three working groups and a review of amber.

The Cabinet Secretary asked for a simpler approach.

Digital proposed a live dashboard showing shelf capacity.

Facilities said the dashboard would have to include the sandwich.

The junior adviser asked whether the sandwich was now more urgent than housing, energy, and procurement reform.

There was a pause.

Legal said it depended whether the sandwich had minutes.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister rehearsed the announcement.

"Today we are putting urgency on a proper footing," the Prime Minister read.

Policy liked footing because it did not specify direction.

Treasury asked whether the shelf was capital spending or furniture.

Legal inserted "where appropriate" after urgency, shelf, and today.

Communications said the public needed to feel that government had gripped the moment.

Digital said the grip could be represented by a progress bar.

The progress bar was 62 percent complete, because nobody wanted to defend either optimism or despair.

The Cabinet Secretary asked what happened when the shelf was full.

Policy said that would trigger the Urgency Overflow Protocol.

Treasury asked whether the protocol had been funded.

Legal asked whether overflowing urgency created a duty to mop.

Communications suggested calling the overflow a pipeline.

The junior adviser said that sounded like putting problems on a shelf, then calling the floor delivery.

Everyone agreed the phrasing was lively and should not leave the room.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the first formal Shelf Prioritisation Panel convened.

The panel's job was to decide which urgent items were urgent enough to remain on the urgent shelf.

A folder marked IMMEDIATE was removed because it had been immediate since April.

A paper marked TODAY was retained because nobody had written which today.

A note marked FINAL FINAL was placed sideways to indicate conditional finality.

The sandwich was escalated to perishable.

Treasury said perishable sounded expensive.

Legal said perishable created a deadline.

Communications said deadlines were strong optics when met and aggressive optics when missed.

Digital tried to photograph the shelf for the dashboard, but the camera detected too many documents and asked whether this was a queue.

The Cabinet Secretary said it was not a queue.

Facilities said people had started standing beside it in order.

Policy suggested renaming it the National Urgency Platform.

The junior adviser said a shelf became a platform when nobody wanted to admit it was full.

The room wrote that down, then immediately placed the note on the shelf.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood beside the National Urgency Shelf and promised a new era of focused delivery.

At that moment, the shelf bracket gave way.

The urgent folders slid gently onto the carpet in descending order of embarrassment.

The sandwich landed on the lectern.

Communications moved to block the cameras, which made the cameras more interested.

Treasury said the collapse might reduce storage costs.

Legal advised against calling it a collapse until a support assessment had determined whether gravity had acted within guidance.

Policy said the incident demonstrated that urgency was moving through the system.

A reporter asked whether the government had literally failed to support its urgent priorities.

The Cabinet Secretary said the shelf had performed an important stress test.

The reporter asked whether it had passed.

Digital checked the dashboard.

It said SHELF STATUS: TRANSITIONING.

The junior adviser picked up the bracket and asked whether this was what implementation looked like after the announcement.

For once, nobody had a holding line ready.

## TAG

By evening, the National Urgency Shelf had been replaced by a sturdier unit called the Immediate Action Rack.

Communications said this showed decisive learning.

Treasury asked whether the old shelf could be reclassified as evidence.

Legal said evidence should not be stored on the rack until the rack's evidence policy was approved.

Digital updated the dashboard to show 100 percent visibility and 0 percent shelf.

The sandwich was given its own workstream.

The junior adviser asked who owned the urgent problems now.

Everyone looked at the rack.

The rack looked back, silently exceeding capacity.

**END**
