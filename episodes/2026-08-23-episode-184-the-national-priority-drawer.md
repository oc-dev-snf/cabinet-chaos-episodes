# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 184
**Title:** The National Priority Drawer
**Date:** 2026-08-23

## COLD OPEN

The department has been told to show that it is taking delivery seriously.

No one knows what delivery is, but everyone agrees it should be visible by lunchtime.

The Minister enters the conference room to find every table covered in folders marked URGENT, IMPORTANT, PRIORITY, IMMEDIATE, and FOR ALIGNMENT.

"Which one is the actual priority?"

The Permanent Secretary gestures to a locked filing cabinet in the corner.

"That is what the National Priority Drawer is designed to establish."

"We have put the priority in a drawer?"

"Temporarily. To prevent premature prioritisation."

The comms director arrives carrying a tray of coloured labels.

"Good news," she says. "We can now photograph seriousness in five shades."

The Minister picks up a red label.

"What does red mean?"

"We are waiting for guidance."

"From whom?"

The room looks at the drawer.

## ACT ONE

The National Priority Drawer began as a practical attempt to stop every team calling its work the top priority.

Within three meetings it had become a cross-government prioritisation discipline, a symbolic repository of strategic intent, and a possible answer to a select committee question that had not yet been asked.

The policy lead explains the mechanism.

"Every proposal goes into the drawer. The drawer creates a neutral space. Then we open it once consensus has formed."

"How does consensus form if the proposals are in a drawer?" asks the Minister.

"Through confidence."

The Permanent Secretary says the drawer is a mature alternative to making choices in public.

"Minister, a priority that has not yet left the drawer cannot disappoint anyone."

A junior official suggests they could simply publish a ranked list of the five most important things, with owners and dates.

The room reacts as if someone has proposed replacing constitutional government with a whiteboard.

The comms director unveils the launch phrase:

**FOCUSED ON WHAT MATTERS**

Legal has cleared it, provided no one asks what matters.

## ACT TWO

The first difficulty is access.

Three teams have requested keys to the drawer, two teams have requested observer status, and one team has asked whether being excluded from the drawer counts as a reputational risk.

The centre wants daily updates on what is inside it.

The Treasury wants to know whether anything inside it costs money.

The implementation unit wants the drawer emptied into a tracker.

The Permanent Secretary refuses.

"Once the contents enter a tracker, they become commitments."

"Aren't commitments the point?" asks the Minister.

"They are one possible interpretation of momentum."

Meanwhile, the comms director has arranged a media visit to demonstrate the drawer "in operational context".

This requires the drawer to contain something impressive but not specific.

The policy lead proposes inserting a blank folder labelled NATIONAL PROGRESS.

"Can a blank folder be progress?"

"It depends how confidently it closes."

The junior official again suggests the ranked list.

This time, someone asks whether it can be folded small enough to fit in the drawer.

## ACT THREE

By the morning of the visit, the department has created a full handling protocol.

Only the Permanent Secretary may unlock the drawer.

Only the Minister may remove a folder.

Only the comms director may describe the folder.

No one may read the folder unless already aligned with its contents.

The press arrive early, which is treated as an operational incident.

The Minister stands beside the filing cabinet.

"Today we are bringing discipline, focus, and grip to the business of government."

The Permanent Secretary unlocks the drawer.

It sticks.

The comms director laughs in a tone designed to imply this was rehearsed.

The Permanent Secretary pulls harder.

The drawer opens suddenly, releasing a compressed avalanche of folders, labels, briefing notes, biscuit wrappers, and one laminated card reading:

**DO NOT CREATE A SYMBOLIC DRAWER**

The Minister bends down and picks up the blank NATIONAL PROGRESS folder.

It is empty except for a sticky note that says, "Ask junior official for actual list."

## CLIMAX

Back in the conference room, the clip is already moving through the news cycle.

One headline calls it "a cabinet full of priorities and no cabinet priority".

Another publishes a helpful explainer on the difference between a drawer and a plan.

The comms director wants to say the incident proves how full the government's agenda is.

The Permanent Secretary wants an urgent review of furniture governance.

The Minister wants the actual list.

The junior official opens a document.

"Five items. Each with an owner, deadline, dependency, and public update date."

The Minister reads it.

"This is alarmingly understandable."

"Thank you."

"I did not say that as criticism."

The Permanent Secretary studies the document with professional concern.

"There is nowhere for ambiguity to gather."

The comms director refreshes her screen.

The public update page is already live.

It has no slogan, no launch photo, and no drawer.

## TAG

At 6:40 PM, the ranked list becomes the department's most shared page.

Stakeholders praise its clarity, which causes a brief internal panic about whether clarity is now expected.

The National Priority Drawer is redesignated as "archival learning infrastructure".

The Minister asks whether the list needs a brand.

The junior official says it needs updating every Friday.

The room goes quiet.

"Every Friday?" says the Permanent Secretary.

"Yes."

"But then people will know whether things have happened."

The Minister looks at the list again.

"That may be the idea."

In the corner, someone gently closes the drawer.

It immediately sticks.
