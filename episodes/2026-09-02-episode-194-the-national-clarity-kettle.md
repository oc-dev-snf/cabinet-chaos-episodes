# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 194
**Title:** The National Clarity Kettle
**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The department has acquired a kettle for the Cabinet anteroom.

It is chrome, compact, and labelled with a small blue sticker:

**CLARITY UNIT - DO NOT BOIL WITHOUT MESSAGE DISCIPLINE**

The Minister stares at it.

"Why does the kettle have a communications protocol?"

The comms director does not look up from the launch grid.

"Because yesterday you said we needed to get the public something hot and simple."

"I meant a policy."

"So did procurement. This is the pilot."

The Permanent Secretary inspects the box.

"It says here the kettle converts uncertainty into steam."

"That is water."

"Water is often the medium through which uncertainty becomes steam."

The junior official arrives carrying mugs, each printed with a different slogan.

One says **DELIVERY**.

One says **LISTENING**.

One says **STRATEGIC PAUSE**.

The Minister picks up the last one.

"Why is strategic pause a mug?"

"It holds things," says the policy lead. "Until they cool down."

The kettle clicks on by itself.

Everyone turns.

The comms director whispers, "Good. It has entered rebuttal mode."

## ACT ONE

The kettle was commissioned after a senior review concluded that the department's announcements lacked warmth.

The review did not define warmth.

It did, however, include a photograph of a meeting where nobody was holding a drink, which was later described as "visually austere".

Comms proposed a hospitality reset.

Policy proposed a clarity framework.

Operations proposed buying the cheapest kettle that could be defended as both.

The Minister is handed the briefing note.

"This says the kettle is stakeholder-facing."

"Only when placed near the door," says the Permanent Secretary.

"And internally transformative?"

"Only when plugged in."

The junior official reads from the risk register.

"Risk one: steam may obscure the message. Risk two: no steam may imply a lack of urgency. Risk three: excessive steam may imply panic."

"Can we just make tea?"

The room absorbs the suggestion with the grave patience reserved for reckless innovation.

The comms director points to the mug selection.

"We need to decide which cup the Minister is seen using. Delivery is active, Listening is humble, Strategic Pause is, regrettably, honest."

The policy lead raises a folder.

"There is also a fourth mug."

It says **CLEAR PLAN**.

The Minister brightens.

"Use that."

"We can't. It leaks."

## ACT TWO

By late morning, the kettle has become the centre of a dispute between clarity, hospitality, and electrical compliance.

Facilities says the plug socket is certified for ordinary hot drinks but not for symbolic transformation.

Legal says a kettle can be called a unit, but only if it does not make decisions.

Finance says the pilot must demonstrate value for money before anyone drinks from it.

Comms says visible drinking before measurable outcomes could look complacent.

The Minister says everyone sounds dehydrated.

The Permanent Secretary suggests convening a Beverage Assurance Group.

"We are not creating a group to authorise boiling water."

"It would not authorise the boiling," says the Permanent Secretary. "It would assure the conditions in which boiling might command confidence."

The junior official has already opened a spreadsheet.

"I've got columns for mug, message, stakeholder temperature, and whether the spoon is aligned to the line."

"Why is there a spoon line?"

"The first photograph showed the spoon pointing left."

"And?"

"Nobody knew whether that meant reform."

The kettle begins to rumble.

The comms director leans in.

"It's building pressure."

"That's what kettles do."

"Exactly. We can use this."

## ACT THREE

The proposed announcement is rewritten around the kettle.

The department will not launch a policy.

It will "set water in motion".

It will not delay decisions.

It will "allow the temperature of consensus to rise".

It will not admit that nobody has agreed the substance.

It will "invite the nation to gather around a shared source of practical heat".

The Minister reads the draft aloud and stops halfway through.

"This is a recipe for waiting."

"It is a narrative of preparation," says the policy lead.

"It is a kettle."

"A very accessible narrative of preparation."

The Permanent Secretary notes that the kettle has, in fairness, achieved cross-departmental alignment.

"Everyone is focused on it."

"Because nobody will let me turn it off."

The junior official checks the wall socket.

"Actually, Minister, it is plugged into the projector extension."

At that moment, the screen behind them wakes up and displays the launch slide:

**A CLEAR PLAN FOR A WARMER DELIVERY ENVIRONMENT**

The kettle clicks off.

The room applauds by accident, because everyone thought the slide had finished.

## CLIMAX

The press pool is waiting downstairs.

The Minister has three minutes, four mugs, one kettle, and no policy line that survives contact with English.

Comms recommends holding the Clear Plan mug by the handle with the crack turned away from camera.

Legal recommends not using the word clear near any object known to leak.

Finance recommends mentioning value.

Policy recommends mentioning momentum.

The Permanent Secretary recommends mentioning that the Minister has convened, considered, and will return.

The Minister fills the Strategic Pause mug.

"Absolutely not," says comms.

"Absolutely yes," says the Minister. "It is the only cup telling the truth."

He walks downstairs.

At the lectern, he places the mug beside the microphone.

"This morning we tested a very small thing and discovered a large one. If a plan cannot survive a kettle, it is not ready for the country. So we are going to stop dressing up hesitation as heat, fix the work, and come back when we can say plainly what we mean."

There is a pause.

A reporter asks whether that means the announcement is delayed.

"Yes," says the Minister. "But clearly."

## TAG

Back upstairs, the junior official empties the kettle and removes the sticker.

The Permanent Secretary watches.

"Careful. Without the label, it may become merely useful."

The junior official plugs it back in.

"That's the hope."

The kettle boils.

Nobody calls it a unit.

**END**
