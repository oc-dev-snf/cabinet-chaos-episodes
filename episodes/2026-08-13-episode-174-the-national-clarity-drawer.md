# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 174
**Title:** The National Clarity Drawer
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office unveiled the National Clarity Drawer.

It was the second drawer down in a grey filing cabinet, labelled CLEAR ANSWERS in laminated tape.

The Cabinet Secretary explained that government had produced plenty of clarity, but it was not always immediately available at the point of questioning.

Policy said the drawer would create a single, accessible location for settled positions.

Legal asked whether settled meant final, current, proposed, preferred, or awaiting clearance.

Communications said the key phrase was "single source of confidence."

Treasury asked whether confidence required ring-fenced funding.

Digital proposed a searchable online version of the drawer, once the drawer's contents had been digitised, tagged, approved, and found.

The junior adviser asked whether the drawer currently contained any clear answers.

The Cabinet Secretary opened it.

Inside were three paperclips, an old badge, and a note reading ASK POLICY.

Policy said this demonstrated excellent cross-referencing.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments were queuing to place answers in the drawer.

Transport submitted a statement saying delivery was moving in the right direction, provided direction was understood as a planning concept.

Health submitted a note confirming that progress had improved since the previous assessment, although the previous assessment had not yet been assessed.

Education asked whether a question could be stored in the clarity drawer until it became an answer.

Legal said storing questions with answers risked creating confusion, unless the questions were clearly marked as pre-answers.

Treasury suggested a one-in, one-out rule for clarity to prevent overcrowding.

Communications said overcrowded clarity was still preferable to visible uncertainty.

Facilities warned that the drawer stuck when opened too quickly.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was a useful reminder that clarity should be accessed responsibly.

The junior adviser asked whether a sticking drawer was really the best metaphor.

Communications said it was not a metaphor if nobody mentioned it outside the building.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister rehearsed the announcement.

"Today we are putting clear answers where people can find them," the Prime Minister read.

Policy liked find, because it implied an active public role.

Treasury asked whether people finding answers would create demand for more answers.

Legal inserted "where appropriate" after clear, answers, people, and find.

Communications wanted a visual.

Facilities brought the drawer into the briefing room, still attached to the cabinet because nobody had established whether detaching it changed its governance status.

Digital placed a QR code on the handle.

The QR code opened a page saying CLARITY CURRENTLY LOADING.

The Cabinet Secretary asked what would happen if a journalist asked for the drawer to be opened.

Legal said opening the drawer would constitute disclosure of drawer-adjacent material.

Communications suggested opening it halfway.

The junior adviser said halfway clarity was just uncertainty with furniture.

Policy wrote down "furniture-enabled confidence" and asked if anyone hated it.

Nobody hated it enough to own the alternative.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Clarity Drawer Assurance Panel reviewed the contents.

The first document was titled DEFINITIVE POSITION.

It contained eight tracked changes and a comment saying "can we soften definitive?"

The second document was titled CURRENT LINE.

It had expired yesterday, but Legal said yesterday could remain current until someone asked in the present tense.

The third document was titled PUBLIC ANSWER.

It was blank apart from the words "awaiting final steers."

Treasury asked whether blank pages counted as lower-cost transparency.

Communications said blankness could be calm if framed properly.

Digital tried to scan the drawer and accidentally created a duplicate folder called CLARITY FINAL USE THIS ONE MAYBE.

The Cabinet Secretary asked which one was authoritative.

Digital said the system was synchronising.

Facilities said the physical drawer had locked itself.

Policy said this created a powerful image of protected certainty.

The junior adviser said it created a locked drawer full of answers nobody could read.

Legal said that was less risky than the other way round.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood beside the filing cabinet and promised a new era of accessible clarity.

A reporter immediately asked what was in the drawer.

Communications smiled with the expression of a person watching a bridge become a policy decision.

The Cabinet Secretary reached for the handle.

The drawer did not open.

Facilities whispered that it needed a small lift and pull.

The Cabinet Secretary lifted and pulled.

The cabinet moved forward.

Treasury noted that the asset was now mobile.

Legal advised against describing the drawer as stuck until mechanical independence had been verified.

Policy said the resistance showed that clarity was robust.

The reporter asked whether the government could access its own clear answers.

Digital refreshed the QR page.

It now read CLARITY AVAILABLE SOON.

The junior adviser found the key taped under the cabinet.

For one bright second, the room believed in resolution.

Then the key opened the bottom drawer, labelled HISTORIC CONFIDENCE.

Inside was last year's plan, still marked urgent.

## TAG

By evening, the National Clarity Drawer had been replaced by a Clear Answer Access Framework.

Communications said this showed the government had moved beyond drawers.

Treasury asked whether the original drawer could be reused for uncertainty.

Legal said uncertainty required separate storage, because it was too easily confused with active policy.

Digital updated the page to say CLARITY HAS BEEN RELOCATED.

The junior adviser asked where.

Everyone looked at the filing cabinet.

The filing cabinet had been moved to a room nobody had booked.

**END**
