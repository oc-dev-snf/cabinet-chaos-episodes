# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 170
**Title:** The National Decision Coaster
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office unveiled the National Decision Coaster.

It was a small cork disc designed to sit beneath every major government decision until the decision was ready to leave a mark.

Communications said it made responsibility feel domestic, relatable, and absorbent.

Policy said it would stop urgent commitments from staining the table before their implications had cooled.

Treasury asked whether the coaster was reusable.

Legal asked whether placing a decision on the coaster constituted taking it, preparing to take it, or merely acknowledging the existence of furniture.

The Cabinet Secretary said the coaster would bring much-needed stability to the space between intention and announcement.

Digital asked whether citizens could track coaster occupancy.

Facilities said the building had hundreds of coasters, but most were from conferences that no longer existed, and one said "Transformation 2019" in a font nobody wished to revive.

The junior adviser asked why a decision needed a coaster at all.

The room agreed it was important not to rush liquids.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, every department wanted coaster time.

Transport said its infrastructure package was too large for a mug but might balance on two coasters with confidence.

Health said its waiting list plan needed a heat-resistant surface and possibly a saucer of empathy.

Education asked whether standards could be placed on the coaster as principles first, with detail added once the tea arrived.

The Home Office requested a black coaster and said no further questions would be helpful.

Treasury proposed a smaller coaster, then a paper coaster, then a notional coaster to be introduced once fiscal conditions allowed.

Communications prepared the line: "The government is putting decisions on a firm footing."

Legal said footing implied movement, and movement implied a route, and a route implied somebody should have checked the map.

The Cabinet Secretary suggested a Coaster Allocation Framework.

Decisions would be graded as HOT, WARM, COOLING, ROOM TEMPERATURE, or LEFT IN MEETING ROOM B.

Digital built a dashboard showing which decisions were currently supported, awaiting support, or ring-shaped.

The junior adviser suggested making a decision without first designing tableware governance.

There was a silence while everyone considered whether that would set an uncontrolled precedent.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the first Decision Coaster rehearsal began.

The Prime Minister sat at the Cabinet table while Communications positioned a mug beside the lectern, then moved it slightly to imply grip.

Policy placed a card on the coaster reading DELIVERY.

Treasury replaced it with AFFORDABLE DELIVERY.

Legal added SUBJECT TO SURFACE CONDITIONS.

Digital attached a QR code that led to a holding page called Is It On The Coaster Yet?

The Cabinet Secretary said the coaster was now carrying too much process.

Communications said process photographed better when it looked intentional.

Policy said intentionality could be briefed as momentum.

Treasury asked whether momentum was included in the spending envelope.

The Prime Minister practised the line.

"This government will not let important decisions drift."

The dashboard updated to: DRIFT BEING MANAGED.

Communications asked if that sounded passive.

Policy changed it to: STABILITY BEING ACTIVELY DEMONSTRATED.

Legal asked whether demonstrated stability required the coaster to remain visible for the whole answer.

The Cabinet Secretary said visibility was the principal delivery mechanism.

The junior adviser asked what happened if a decision spilled over the edge.

Digital said edge cases would be handled in phase two.

Treasury said there would be no phase two.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Interdepartmental Coaster Board met in the room with the least threatening table.

Transport arrived with a model bridge and a laminated coaster compatibility assessment.

Health arrived with a chart that had been softened by placing it under a mug of peppermint tea.

Education arrived with a draft definition of "improvement" printed on paper thick enough to look official.

The Home Office arrived with its own coaster in an envelope.

Treasury arrived with a coaster-shaped gap in the budget.

Communications said the gap looked negative.

Treasury said it was disciplined.

Policy suggested calling it a "reserved circle of future action."

Legal warned that a circle could be interpreted as a promise to return to the same point.

The Cabinet Secretary asked whether the public would understand coaster-led decision management.

Communications said the public understood tea, stains, and waiting for someone else to clear a table.

Digital reported that the coaster tracker had strong engagement, mostly from people trying to work out whether their local concern was HOT, COOLING, or ABSORBED.

The junior adviser said the coaster did not make decisions clearer; it simply gave indecision a place setting.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was unfair to a cross-government stabilisation object.

## CLIMAX

The press conference began with the National Decision Coaster placed carefully beside the Prime Minister's notes.

"This government is taking practical steps to support serious decisions," the Prime Minister said.

The coaster label read: SERIOUSNESS SUPPORTED.

A reporter asked which decision was currently on it.

Communications signalled readiness.

Policy signalled sequencing.

Treasury signalled that readiness and sequencing were both subject to affordability.

Legal signalled that naming the decision might prematurely moisten the record.

The Prime Minister said the coaster represented calm, grip, and responsible stewardship of the national table.

The dashboard updated to: NATIONAL TABLE UNDER REVIEW.

Another reporter asked whether the government had created a prop to avoid announcing anything.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was a superficial reading of a practical assurance platform with stain-prevention benefits.

Digital announced that citizens would soon be able to sign up for coaster alerts, including "decision placed", "decision lifted", "decision cooling", and "surface wiped for future use."

The junior adviser said the coaster had achieved something rare: every department could see the decision, nobody had to own the decision, and the only thing absorbing pressure was cork.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Decision Coaster would give the public confidence that important choices were not being rushed, spilled, or left directly on polished wood.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested matching placemats.

Treasury approved one napkin to be shared across government.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's line could be "steady hands, steady table."

Legal said only if nobody inferred lunch.
