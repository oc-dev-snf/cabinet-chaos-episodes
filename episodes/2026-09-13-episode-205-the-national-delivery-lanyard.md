# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 205
**Title:** The National Delivery Lanyard
**Date:** 2026-09-13
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister enters the Cabinet room to find every chair fitted with a lanyard.

Not every person.

Every chair.

Each lanyard holds a laminated pass reading **AUTHORISED TO DELIVER**.

"Why has the furniture been security-cleared?"

The Permanent Secretary looks pleased with the question.

"It is the National Delivery Lanyard."

"It is an office necklace for a chair."

"It demonstrates access to outcomes."

"The chair has access to my knees."

The comms director straightens the pass on the Minister's seat.

"The visual says delivery is in the room."

"So is the carpet. Is the carpet delivering?"

The policy lead checks a spreadsheet.

"Only if Facilities has signed the participation protocol."

The junior official raises a hand.

"My chair's pass says visitor."

The Permanent Secretary nods.

"Your chair has not completed the e-learning."

The Minister looks around the table.

"We have credentialed the furniture before briefing the people."

"A strong start," says the comms director.

"For burglary."

## ACT ONE

The lanyard began as a badge for officials attending a delivery summit.

By breakfast it had become a cross-government access model.

By nine forty-five it had gained a framework, a glossary, and a small plastic reel described as an accountability extender.

The Minister reads the briefing.

"The lanyard makes delivery visible, wearable, and auditable."

"By whom?"

"By anyone close enough to read the badge," says the policy lead.

"That is not audit. That is squinting."

Security objects that **AUTHORISED TO DELIVER** could allow unauthorised people to appear delivered.

Legal objects that delivered to whom has not been specified.

Finance objects to the word authorised on cost grounds.

The comms director proposes replacing it with **ENABLED TO PROGRESS**.

"That says nothing."

"Exactly. It travels better."

The Permanent Secretary suggests a lanyard alignment exercise.

"Everyone already has one."

"Yes, Minister, but some are facing inward."

The junior official turns his pass around.

It now reads **TEMPORARY OBSERVER OF IMPLEMENTATION**.

"That feels worse."

"It is more precise," says the Permanent Secretary.

"Precision is not a synonym for help."

## ACT TWO

Departments begin submitting lanyard requests.

Transport wants orange for things that are nearly moving.

Health wants antimicrobial clips.

Education asks whether pupils should be issued child-sized ambition holders.

The Treasury asks whether departments can share one lanyard on a rota.

The policy lead unveils the Access To Outcomes Matrix.

Green means authorised to deliver.

Amber means authorised to discuss delivery.

Red means awaiting authorisation to be near delivery.

Grey means the pass printer is reflecting strategically.

The Minister taps the matrix.

"Where is the column for doing the thing?"

"Doing the thing sits behind access," says the Permanent Secretary.

"Why?"

"So it can be properly controlled."

"You have put a turnstile in front of reality."

Comms wants a launch photo with ministers holding their passes toward the camera.

Legal says visible passes may expose operational details.

Security says hidden passes defeat the pass.

Finance says lanyards visible from the front and back may count as two communications surfaces.

The junior official's retractable reel snaps and flings his badge into the fruit bowl.

Everyone pauses.

The comms director writes **organic stakeholder engagement** in the margin.

## ACT THREE

By early afternoon the room is divided by credential level.

People with blue lanyards may speak about delivery.

People with green lanyards may refer to delivery in the future tense.

People with red lanyards may nod during delivery but not imply causation.

The Minister discovers his pass has expired at lunch.

"How can I chair the meeting?"

"You can chair it," says the Permanent Secretary. "You just cannot access the chair."

"The chair is wearing the valid pass."

"Correct."

The Minister stands beside the chair like a man negotiating with upholstery.

A junior press officer enters.

"The broadcasters are asking whether the lanyard is the policy."

The comms director looks injured.

"It is the gateway to the policy."

"They are also asking where the policy is."

Silence.

The policy lead checks the folder.

"Behind the gateway."

The Minister closes his eyes.

"We have invented a door without a wall."

The junior official retrieves his badge from beside the bananas.

It now has a sticker on it reading **FAST TRACK**.

"Who put that there?"

No one answers.

The Permanent Secretary makes a note to establish a fruit bowl governance trail.

## CLIMAX

The press conference is moved to the atrium because the lanyards need natural light.

Officials form a backdrop in descending order of authorisation.

This creates a visible gradient from confidence to laminated doubt.

A reporter asks whether delivery has improved since the lanyards were introduced.

The Permanent Secretary offers the prepared line.

"The National Delivery Lanyard provides a clear, consistent, portable signal of implementation readiness."

The Minister looks at the passes, the clips, the colour codes, and the staff trying not to rotate out of compliance.

Then his own lanyard reel breaks.

The badge drops to the floor.

He leaves it there.

"Delivery is not a pass around your neck. It is the unglamorous act of making a promise survive contact with money, time, law, and people who have to use the result. If a badge is clearer than the plan, the badge is not proof of delivery. It is evidence that the plan has gone missing."

The comms director whispers that the dropped badge photographs well.

The Minister points at it.

"Good. Caption it honestly."

The junior official checks his notebook.

"What should it say?"

"Access denied."

## TAG

Back in the Cabinet room, Facilities removes the lanyards from the chairs.

One remains caught around the table leg.

The Permanent Secretary says it should stay until the decommissioning protocol is agreed.

The Minister looks under the table.

The pass reads **AUTHORISATION PENDING**.

"For what?"

The junior official peers over.

"Standing up, apparently."

The Minister sits on the edge of the table.

"At last. A policy with posture."

**END**
