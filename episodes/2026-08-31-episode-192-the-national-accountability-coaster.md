# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 192
**Title:** The National Accountability Coaster
**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Cabinet room has received a new set of coasters.

They are square, cork-backed, and printed with the departmental crest above the words:

**PLEASE PLACE ACCOUNTABILITY HERE**

The Minister pauses with a mug halfway to the table.

"Why does accountability need a coaster?"

The Permanent Secretary adjusts one by two millimetres.

"To prevent ring marks, Minister."

"On the table?"

"On the decision."

The comms director picks one up and studies it against the light.

"It is tactile. Voters like tactile. It suggests grip."

The junior official counts the set.

"There are only six."

"That is intentional," says the policy lead. "Accountability must be prioritised."

The Minister looks around the room at twelve people and six coasters.

"So half of us are allowed to be accountable?"

The Permanent Secretary smiles.

"Half may be visibly accountable. The others can support the conditions for accountability."

## ACT ONE

The coasters were commissioned after last week's review concluded that accountability had become "too diffuse to rest a hot drink on".

Facilities objected that ordinary coasters already existed.

Policy replied that ordinary coasters lacked a governance function.

Comms requested a colour that looked serious without implying blame.

Legal approved charcoal.

The Minister is shown a briefing pack titled A Stable Surface For Responsibility.

"This is fourteen pages about putting mugs down."

"With respect," says the policy lead, "it is thirteen pages about public confidence. Page fourteen is wipe-clean guidance."

The coasters are designed to sit beneath any decision that has been announced before being fully owned.

"What happens when the owner is known?"

"Then the coaster can be removed."

"And has that ever happened?"

The Permanent Secretary turns a page.

"The pilot is ongoing."

## ACT TWO

By lunchtime, the coasters have caused an interdepartmental dispute.

Delivery wants one for the timetable.

Finance wants one for the caveat.

Digital wants one for a dashboard tile showing whether the dashboard is ready to show tiles.

Legal wants two: one for the risk and one for the risk of describing the risk.

The comms director places a coaster beneath the draft announcement.

"This reads better with a square under it."

"It does not become true because it has furniture," says the Minister.

"No, but it becomes less slippery."

The junior official has made a list of open questions.

"We still need to know who signs off the pilot, who publishes the measures, and who answers when the measures show the pilot has not done the thing the announcement says it is doing."

The room goes quiet.

The Permanent Secretary reaches for a coaster.

"That question may require a surface."

"It requires an answer."

"An answer should not be placed directly on untreated process."

## ACT THREE

An accountability allocation session is convened.

Each team is invited to place a coaster beneath the area for which it accepts responsibility.

Delivery places one under "implementation readiness", then clarifies this does not include implementation.

Finance places one under "funding pathway", then clarifies this does not include funds.

Digital places one under "future transparency", then clarifies this does not include current transparency.

Legal places one under "defensible language", which everyone agrees is the most accurate object in the room.

The Minister keeps the final coaster in front of him.

"What exactly am I meant to put on this?"

The comms director brightens.

"Leadership."

"Leadership is not a beverage."

"No, but it steams."

The junior official slides over the original decision note. It contains three crossed-out owners, two pending approvals, and one sentence that says "Minister to confirm".

The Minister reads it twice.

"So this coaster is for the bit where I stop asking who owns the decision and accept that I do."

The Permanent Secretary considers the formulation.

"That is one available interpretation."

"Good. I am available."

## CLIMAX

The room tries to help.

Policy offers to draft an ownership framework.

Delivery offers to create a milestone for identifying the milestone owner.

Finance offers to confirm whether the budget line is a budget line or a hopeful paragraph with numbers nearby.

Legal offers to remove the word "guarantee" from every sentence in the building.

The Minister puts his mug firmly on the last coaster.

"No. We are going to write the decision down."

The junior official opens a clean page.

The Minister dictates:

**THE PILOT STARTS ON MONDAY.**

**DELIVERY OWNS THE FIRST MONTH.**

**FINANCE CONFIRMS THE MONEY BY FRIDAY.**

**DIGITAL PUBLISHES ONE MEASURE PEOPLE CAN UNDERSTAND.**

**I WILL ANSWER FOR IT AT THE NEXT STATEMENT.**

Comms looks pained.

"That is quite exposed."

"It is a coaster," says the Minister. "Exposure is the entire point."

For a moment, nobody moves.

Then Legal quietly removes three adjectives.

Finance confirms a number.

Delivery names a person.

Digital changes "interactive performance environment" to "page".

The coaster leaves a faint damp square around the decision.

It is the first clear mark anyone has made all day.

## TAG

The announcement goes out with fewer claims and more names.

The press notice is described internally as "unsettlingly capable of being checked".

The Permanent Secretary orders a protective sleeve for the coaster, in case accountability becomes a recurring requirement.

By evening, the junior official has added a handwritten label to the box:

**WASH AFTER USE**

Underneath, someone has written:

**REUSE BEFORE LAUNCH**
