# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 200
**Title:** The National Continuity Tray
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The Minister arrives to find a silver tray in the centre of the Cabinet table.

On it sit three folders, two pens, a bell, a laminated card, and a small empty bowl labelled **URGENT BUT NOT YET OWNED**.

The Permanent Secretary stands beside it with the solemn pride of a museum guide.

"What is that?"

"The National Continuity Tray."

"Continuity of what?"

"Government."

The comms director is already adjusting the angle for photographs.

"It says calm, stable, ready."

"It says someone has brought room service to a constitutional crisis."

The policy lead points to the laminated card.

"The tray ensures every priority remains visibly in hand."

The Minister looks at the folders.

"Whose hand?"

Nobody answers.

The junior official enters carrying a second tray.

"Facilities says this is the tray for trays requiring tray governance."

The Permanent Secretary nods.

"Good. Put it beside the escalation coasters."

## ACT ONE

The Continuity Tray began as a practical attempt to stop papers being lost between meetings.

By 9:15 it had become a cross-government operating model.

By 9:40 it had acquired a mission statement.

By 10:05 it had been described as "a physical expression of joined-up delivery" by someone who had never joined up a plug.

The Minister reads the briefing.

"The tray allows departments to place live issues into a shared space of accountable visibility."

"That means on the table," says the junior official.

"Yes," says the Permanent Secretary. "But with governance."

Finance wants to know whether a tray counts as capital expenditure if it carries ambitions.

Legal warns that the empty bowl must not imply the existence of unowned urgency.

"But it literally says urgent but not yet owned."

"Exactly. It is too clear."

Comms proposes changing the label to **EMERGING HANDOVER OPPORTUNITY**.

The Minister picks up one of the pens.

"Does this work?"

The policy lead winces.

"That is the decision pen."

"So?"

"It should only be used once the tray has reached consensus."

The Minister puts it back.

"We have invented stationery with veto power."

## ACT TWO

By lunchtime, departments are competing for tray space.

Health submits a folder too large to fit.

Transport sends a model traffic cone "for scale."

Treasury contributes a note saying it supports the tray in principle but not the tray's contents, weight, polish, or implied fiscal glide path.

The Permanent Secretary convenes the Tray Allocation Panel.

Comms asks whether the panel can stand around the tray looking purposeful.

"Only if no one touches anything," says legal.

The Minister looks at the agenda.

"Item one: agree whether the tray is a process or a platform."

"It may be both."

"It is neither. It is a tray."

The policy lead unveils a dashboard showing tray occupancy.

Green means the issue is on the tray.

Amber means the issue is near the tray.

Red means the issue has fallen behind the tray and is now being treated as context.

The junior official has discovered that one folder has been moved between trays so often that nobody remembers what it is about.

The Permanent Secretary studies the label.

"It says 'decision pending.'"

"About what?"

"Possibly the tray."

## ACT THREE

The launch rehearsal collapses when the bell is rung.

Everyone freezes.

The Minister asks what the bell means.

The policy lead checks the protocol.

"It signals that continuity has been activated."

"Was continuity not already active?"

"It was available."

Legal says activation may trigger responsibilities.

Finance asks whether responsibilities have a unit cost.

Comms says the public will like the bell if it sounds reassuring.

The junior official says it currently sounds like a hotel asking for soup.

A dispute breaks out over whether the tray should move to the Minister or the Minister should move to the tray.

The Permanent Secretary prefers the second option.

"It preserves institutional gravity."

"It preserves the tray's travel budget," says the Minister.

The policy lead tries to demonstrate the intended handover motion.

She lifts the folder from the tray, turns to the Minister, hesitates because no owner has been agreed, turns back, and replaces it exactly where it was.

Comms applauds.

"That looked controlled."

"That was indecision with wrist action."

The junior official writes: **handover may require recipient**.

## CLIMAX

In the press room, the tray gleams under the lights.

The Minister stands behind it like a man selling a raffle prize he has just been told is policy.

The first reporter asks whether the government is seriously presenting a tray as evidence of delivery.

The comms director mouths the phrase accountable visibility.

The Permanent Secretary gives the smallest possible nod.

The Minister looks down at the empty bowl.

"A tray is useful," he says, "if it helps you carry things from one place to another. It is not a decision. It is not a plan. It is not continuity. If something urgent has no owner, putting it in a bowl does not make it less urgent. It just gives the problem nicer lighting."

A reporter asks what happens to the Continuity Tray now.

"We are keeping the tray," says the Minister.

Comms exhales.

"For biscuits."

The room goes quiet.

The junior official places a plain sheet on the lectern.

At the top it says:

**THING. PERSON. WHEN.**

The Minister signs it with an ordinary pen.

## TAG

Back in the Cabinet room, the silver tray holds a packet of digestives and one working pen.

The Permanent Secretary says this is a proportionate use of departmental assets.

The Minister takes a biscuit.

The junior official writes the first owner next to the first problem.

For once, nothing needs a bell.
