# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 172
**Title:** The National Accountability Lanyard
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office introduced the National Accountability Lanyard.

It was navy, adjustable, and printed with the words RESPONSIBILITY HOLDER in a font described by Communications as "firm but not prosecutable."

The idea was simple: during any meeting, the person wearing the lanyard would be visibly responsible for the outcome.

Policy asked whether the wearer would also be able to make decisions.

Legal said that would require a separate authority assessment.

Treasury asked whether responsibility could be shared by passing the lanyard between departments at agreed intervals.

Digital proposed a QR code so citizens could scan the lanyard and see who was currently accountable, provided the page had finished loading.

The Cabinet Secretary said the public wanted clarity.

Communications said the public wanted clarity in principle, but not necessarily enough clarity to generate a named person before breakfast.

The junior adviser asked whether accountability that had to be worn on a string was still accountability.

The room agreed that was a valuable philosophical point and assigned it to a subgroup.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, the first Accountability Lanyard Allocation Meeting had begun.

Transport said it was happy to wear the lanyard for delivery, but only while delivery meant readiness to begin movement.

Health said it could wear the lanyard for patient experience, provided experience excluded buildings, appointments, waiting times, and anything involving chairs.

Education offered to wear it during announcements and remove it during implementation, on the grounds that these were distinct learning environments.

Treasury said it could not wear the lanyard near numbers because observers might assume financial ownership.

Legal advised that the lanyard should not be worn by anyone sitting down, standing up, leaving the room, entering the room, or appearing in a photograph next to a folder.

Communications suggested a rotating model, where every department wore the lanyard for six minutes and then issued a statement praising collective responsibility.

The Cabinet Secretary asked who would be responsible for the rotation.

There was a long pause.

Facilities offered a coat hook.

The junior adviser said the coat hook had become the most accountable participant in government.

Legal asked whether the hook had been briefed.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister rehearsed the launch.

The National Accountability Lanyard lay on the lectern like a small ceremonial warning.

Communications handed over the approved line.

"This government is strengthening visible responsibility across public life."

Policy said "visible" was useful because it did not promise practical.

Treasury said "strengthening" should be cost-neutral if no one defined the previous strength.

Legal inserted "where appropriate" after responsibility.

Digital asked whether the lanyard could be livestreamed.

Communications said the optics of an empty lanyard hanging in frame might suggest nobody was in charge.

The Cabinet Secretary said nobody was saying nobody was in charge.

Legal said that depended on the definition of in charge.

Policy suggested placing the lanyard on a neutral display stand and referring to it as a symbol.

Treasury liked symbol, because symbols rarely submit invoices.

The junior adviser asked whether the public might notice that the responsibility lanyard was being protected from responsible use.

Communications said the public noticed many things and government could not be expected to validate all of them in real time.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Interdepartmental Lanyard Board reviewed operational risks.

Risk one: the wearer might be asked a direct question.

Risk two: the wearer might answer it.

Risk three: the answer might be useful enough to create follow-up demand.

Legal proposed a three-stage protection system: wear, acknowledge, defer.

Policy wanted a guidance note explaining that accountability meant a clear path to future clarification.

Treasury wanted to know whether future clarification could be scheduled after the spending review.

Digital demonstrated the QR code.

It opened a page saying CURRENTLY IDENTIFYING THE CURRENT HOLDER.

Communications called this honest.

Legal called it exposed.

Treasury called it refreshingly inexpensive.

The Cabinet Secretary asked whether the lanyard could be worn by a team.

Facilities produced a longer lanyard.

Six officials tried to stand inside it at once, forming a small administrative circle that could move only sideways.

Communications said the image was unfortunate but accurate.

The junior adviser said it was the first time collective responsibility had been visible to the naked eye.

Nobody disagreed, because nobody could turn around.

## CLIMAX

The launch began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister approached the lectern.

The lanyard was not on the lectern.

It was not on the display stand.

It was not on the coat hook.

Digital checked the QR page, which said the current holder was "pending a short internal alignment exercise."

Communications went pale in a controlled and message-compliant way.

Policy said perhaps the missing lanyard proved that accountability was mobile.

Treasury said if it was missing, it should not appear in the asset register until value had been established.

Legal said nobody should speculate on whether the lanyard had been lost, mislaid, reallocated, or made operationally unavailable.

A reporter asked who was accountable for losing the accountability lanyard.

The Cabinet Secretary stepped forward and said the government took the matter extremely seriously.

The reporter asked who "the government" meant.

The room looked at the empty display stand.

The junior adviser removed their visitor pass, wrote TEMPORARY ANSWER on the back, and hung it on the stand.

For seven seconds, the system worked.

Then Communications asked who had authorised the handwriting.

## TAG

By evening, the National Accountability Lanyard had been found in a folder marked FINAL VERSION DO NOT WEAR.

Legal said this was reassuring because it showed the item had remained within process.

Treasury asked whether non-use counted as preservation.

Digital updated the QR page to say RESPONSIBILITY IS BEING LOCATED.

Communications drafted a statement: "Accountability remains clearly visible within the wider framework."

The junior adviser asked where.

Everyone pointed at the folder.

The folder was empty again.
