# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 193
**Title:** The National Readiness Doormat
**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

The department has installed a new doormat outside the Cabinet room.

It is navy, bristly, and printed in white capitals:

**WELCOME TO DELIVERY**

Nobody steps on it.

The Minister stands in the corridor with one shoe hovering over the slogan.

"Am I welcoming delivery, or is delivery welcoming me?"

The Permanent Secretary consults the installation note.

"The mat is designed to create a threshold of confidence."

"It is designed to remove mud."

"Mud is a form of stakeholder uncertainty."

The comms director crouches beside it.

"It photographs well from above. It says we are open, active, and prepared to have shoes."

The junior official peers through the Cabinet room door.

"The meeting is already twenty minutes late."

"Excellent," says the policy lead. "That means readiness has begun before attendance."

The Minister lowers his foot.

The doormat slides forward six inches and wedges itself under the door.

Inside, twelve senior officials are now trapped behind a welcome message.

## ACT ONE

The doormat was ordered after a cross-government audit found that too many initiatives were entering delivery "without a clear sense of arrival".

Facilities proposed signage.

Comms proposed a launch moment.

Policy proposed a threshold.

Procurement combined all three and bought a mat.

The Minister is shown the readiness pack. Its cover features a photograph of the doormat at a flattering angle, taken before anybody tried to use it.

"This pack says the doormat is phase one."

"Correct," says the policy lead.

"Of what?"

"Phase two."

The Permanent Secretary explains that the doormat is not merely symbolic. It has a measurable function.

"Each person entering the room must pause, acknowledge delivery, and remove any residue from previous positions."

"Previous positions?"

"Statements, promises, uncosted enthusiasm."

The junior official checks the pilot checklist.

"We still need confirmation of ownership, metrics, timetable, public line, contingency line, and whether the door can open."

The comms director raises a hand.

"Could we say the door is being held shut by the strength of interest?"

The Minister looks at the mat, the door, and the faces visible through the glass panel.

"We could also move the mat."

There is a silence reserved for suggestions that involve the object rather than the narrative.

## ACT TWO

By mid-morning, the doormat has become a cross-cutting workstream.

Facilities will not move it without a removal request.

Procurement will not issue a removal request because the mat is still within its settling-in period.

Legal advises that removing a welcome message could be interpreted as withdrawing welcome.

Comms advises that withdrawing welcome during a readiness week would be unhelpful.

Delivery advises that readiness week was meant to start after the readiness meeting, which cannot start because the readiness mat is preventing access to the readiness room.

The Minister rubs his temples.

"Has anyone tried lifting it?"

The junior official lifts one corner.

An alarm chirps.

Everyone freezes.

The Permanent Secretary reads the small print on the underside.

"Ah. It has a confidence sensor."

"Why does a doormat need a confidence sensor?"

"To ensure confidence is not bypassed."

The comms director is already typing.

"This is good. It shows we have safeguards."

"It shows we have a carpet that reports dissent."

"That is one framing."

From inside the Cabinet room, the finance director taps on the glass and holds up a note.

It says:

**CAN WE BE READY REMOTELY?**

The policy lead considers this.

"Only if the threshold can be virtually respected."

## ACT THREE

An emergency threshold board is convened in the corridor.

No one is sure whether the corridor counts as pre-delivery, delivery-adjacent, or the first operational band of delivery itself.

The Minister asks for options.

Option one: leave the mat in place and describe the delay as evidence of rigorous entry controls.

Option two: move the meeting to another room and install a photograph of the mat in the minutes.

Option three: cancel readiness week and announce a listening period about thresholds.

Option four: open the door.

The Permanent Secretary gently circles option four.

"This has dependencies."

"On what?"

"On accepting that the thing blocking progress is ours."

The junior official kneels beside the doormat with a screwdriver borrowed from maintenance.

"It is fixed to the floor with temporary screws."

"Temporary?"

"They require a permanent decision to remove."

The Minister studies the screws.

"Who authorised them?"

Procurement checks the order.

"The approval line says 'to support frictionless delivery'."

Everyone looks at the mat.

The mat looks back, if a rectangle can look smug.

The Minister says, "Fine. I authorise friction."

## CLIMAX

The corridor becomes suddenly practical.

Facilities produces a tool kit.

Legal drafts a one-sentence note confirming that moving a doormat is not a constitutional event.

Finance agrees that the cost of not moving it is now larger than the cost of admitting it exists.

Comms asks whether anyone has footage of the Minister taking decisive action near an entrance.

"No cameras," says the Minister.

"But the mat has become a symbol."

"Then it can symbolise being moved."

The junior official removes the final screw.

The Minister lifts the doormat, steps into the Cabinet room, and places it inside the door.

"There. Delivery is welcome after the meeting has actually started."

The room exhales.

The first agenda item is readiness.

The Minister crosses it out.

"We are not measuring readiness by how many things we put in front of the door. We are measuring it by whether the door opens."

Delivery names a first task.

Finance names a ceiling.

Legal removes three ornamental cautions.

Comms changes "transformational entrance architecture" to "meeting began".

For once, the minutes fit on one page.

## TAG

By evening, every department has requested a doormat of its own.

Policy warns against uncontrolled threshold proliferation.

Procurement proposes a framework agreement for symbolic floor-based assurance products.

The Permanent Secretary files the incident under "learning".

The junior official tapes a small handwritten sign above the Cabinet room door:

**PUSH**

Underneath, someone has added:

**THEN DO THE THING**
