# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 180
**Title:** The National Priority Clipboard
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Cabinet Office introduced the National Priority Clipboard.

It was black, heavy, and carried by a senior official who had been told not to look hurried.

The Cabinet Secretary explained that priorities had become too dispersed and would now be attached to a single approved surface.

Communications said the clipboard gave the public something firm to believe in.

Policy said it created a visible spine for delivery.

Treasury asked whether the spine was capital or operational.

Legal asked whether clipping a document implied possession, endorsement, custody, or unfortunate proximity.

Digital offered to build a real-time clipboard occupancy dashboard.

The junior adviser asked what made this clipboard national.

The Cabinet Secretary said it had been through procurement twice.

Everyone nodded.

The clipboard was placed in the middle of the table.

No one was allowed to touch it until the handling protocol had been prioritized.

## ACT ONE

By 08:30, departments had begun submitting items for the clipboard.

Transport brought a timetable recovery plan and asked whether the clip could hold delayed optimism.

Health brought a waiting list update printed on paper so thin it seemed to apologize.

Education brought a reform note with three titles, none of which had been selected in case selection caused division.

Treasury said the clipboard could hold five priorities, or seven if two were described as context.

Legal circulated advice confirming that context was not legally identical to content, unless read aloud.

Communications wanted a photograph of the Prime Minister gripping the clipboard with calm urgency.

Policy warned that gripping might imply ownership.

Digital's first dashboard displayed a rectangle and the message CLIPBOARD STATE BEING ESTABLISHED.

The Cabinet Secretary asked why the rectangle was blue.

Digital said blue tested well with people who wanted things to exist.

The junior adviser suggested writing the priorities directly on a list.

The room went quiet.

The Cabinet Secretary said lists were how governments accidentally finished things in the wrong order.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Prime Minister arrived for a briefing on the clipboard's strategic potential.

"The country expects focus," the Prime Minister said.

Communications wrote down focus.

Policy asked whether focus was a priority, an operating principle, or a tone.

Treasury asked whether tone needed funding.

Legal said focus could be used if accompanied by an appropriate blur.

The Cabinet Secretary proposed that the clipboard should contain only the top priority.

Every department immediately claimed to be holding it.

Transport said movement was the first duty of government.

Health said capacity was the first duty of government.

Education said future capacity moving responsibly was the first duty of government.

Treasury said the first duty of government was not creating a second duty without costings.

Communications suggested a compromise: the clipboard would hold a priority about priorities.

Policy called it elegant.

Legal called it defensible.

The junior adviser called it a clipboard with one piece of paper on it.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was the elegance.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Priority Clipboard Handling Board convened to decide who could carry it into the press room.

Communications wanted the Prime Minister to carry it.

Legal warned that carrying could be interpreted as personal acceptance of all clipped matter.

Treasury preferred a neutral official because neutral officials depreciated more predictably.

Policy suggested the clipboard be carried by process.

Facilities asked how tall process was.

Digital proposed mounting a tablet to the clipboard so its status could be monitored.

Treasury asked whether the tablet could be rented.

Legal asked whether the clipboard would remain a clipboard if it became partly tablet.

Communications said modern government was about hybrid grip.

The Cabinet Secretary liked hybrid grip very much and asked for it to be included in the line.

The junior adviser noticed the clip was empty.

Policy said emptiness demonstrated capacity.

Communications said capacity photographed badly.

Someone slid a blank sheet under the clip and titled it NATIONAL PRIORITY HOLDING POSITION.

The room relaxed.

## CLIMAX

The press conference began at 13:00.

The Prime Minister stood beside the National Priority Clipboard, which rested on a lectern under its own small light.

"Today we are putting the country's priorities where everyone can see them," the Prime Minister said.

A reporter asked what the priority was.

Communications looked at Policy.

Policy looked at Legal.

Legal checked the sheet.

Treasury checked whether the sheet had been double-sided.

The Cabinet Secretary whispered that the priority was to establish a clear priority.

The Prime Minister repeated this with emphasis.

The reporter asked whether that meant there was no current priority.

Communications said the priority was current because it had been announced today.

Digital refreshed the dashboard.

It changed from CLIPBOARD STATE BEING ESTABLISHED to PRIORITY VISIBILITY ACTIVE.

The clip sprang open.

The blank sheet lifted, turned over, and revealed a handwritten note from Facilities.

PLEASE RETURN CLIPBOARD BY 3PM.

There was a pause.

The Prime Minister said responsible government meant returning important tools to the public estate.

Communications called it a powerful symbol of stewardship.

Treasury asked whether late return fees applied.

## TAG

By evening, the National Priority Clipboard had been redesignated as the Priority Visibility Platform.

Digital added phases for clipped, unclipped, awaiting clip, clip-adjacent, and held in reserve.

Legal drafted guidance on accidental prioritization through stationery.

Treasury imposed a one-sheet discipline across government, with exemptions for summaries explaining the discipline.

Communications released a photograph of the clipboard from an angle that made it look decisive.

Policy proposed a touring programme so departments could stand near it in alphabetical order.

The Cabinet Secretary said the exercise had brought real grip to the centre.

The junior adviser asked where the actual priorities had gone.

Everyone looked at the clipboard.

It was back in the stationery cupboard, exactly on time.
