# THE THICK OF ITCH

**Episode:** 166
**Title:** The National Context Box
**Genre:** Original political satire

---

## COLD OPEN

At 07:00, the Prime Minister announced the National Context Box.

It was described as a compact, transparent container into which every government statement would place the facts needed to understand why the statement was not quite saying what it appeared to be saying.

Communications said the public wanted context.

Policy said the public wanted context only after the headline had already landed.

Treasury asked whether context could be smaller.

Legal asked whether the box was advisory, binding, decorative, or discoverable.

Digital asked whether it had an API.

Facilities asked whether anyone meant an actual box.

The Cabinet Secretary said the Context Box would restore trust by showing that the government had nothing to hide except the material outside the box.

The first draft contained four approved labels:

IMPORTANT BACKGROUND.

RELEVANT CONSIDERATIONS.

OPERATIONAL COMPLEXITY.

PLEASE READ AFTER BEING REASSURED.

The junior adviser asked whether context was supposed to make things clearer.

The room agreed clarity was one possible interpretation of context.

## ACT ONE

By 08:15, departments had begun submitting examples.

Transport placed a rail punctuality claim in the Context Box with a note excluding weather, engineering, staffing, leaves, and trains.

Health submitted a performance chart whose context explained that numbers were best understood as part of a wider human story with no axis.

Education submitted a literacy pledge and asked whether the box could include a glossary for pledge.

The Home Office submitted a sealed folder and said the context was national security.

Treasury submitted a one-line statement saying all context must fit within existing envelopes.

Communications proposed putting the box next to the Prime Minister at the lectern.

Policy said that risked the box becoming the story.

Communications said that was why the box needed branding.

Legal said the phrase transparent box created a reasonable expectation that people could see inside it.

The Cabinet Secretary proposed frosted transparency.

Digital suggested an online expandable panel.

Treasury asked whether expanded context would count as new spending.

The junior adviser asked whether, if the context explained the announcement away, the announcement should be changed.

The Cabinet Secretary said that was exactly the sort of context the box was designed to contain.

## ACT TWO

At 10:00, the Context Box pilot began.

Every ministerial line now required a Primary Statement, a Supporting Context, a Context Context, and a Box Handling Note.

The Prime Minister rehearsed the launch.

"We are making record progress," the Prime Minister said.

The box beside the lectern displayed: RECORD DEPENDS ON COMPARATOR PERIOD.

Communications asked for warmer phrasing.

Policy changed it to: RECORD PROGRESS SHOULD BE UNDERSTOOD IN A BROADER TEMPORAL FRAME.

Treasury approved broader because it did not commit to wider.

Legal asked whether temporal frame had previously been defined.

Digital said the public could click through to a temporal frame explainer as soon as the explainer had context.

The next line was: "Families will feel the benefits soon."

The box displayed: SOON IS SUBJECT TO DELIVERY CONDITIONS.

Communications said the box was interrupting the emotional arc.

Policy said the emotional arc needed to survive contact with the audit trail.

The Cabinet Secretary said the solution was not less context, but better sequencing of opacity.

## ACT THREE

At noon, the Context Box became too full.

Transport had added route-specific caveats.

Health had added clinical nuance.

Education had added three footnotes about the meaning of core.

The Home Office had added a second sealed folder explaining why the first sealed folder could not be explained.

Treasury had added an instruction to remove all non-essential context and then marked every removal as essential savings.

The box now contained so much clarification that the original announcement could no longer be located.

Digital proposed a search function.

Legal asked whether searchable context increased the risk that someone would search it.

Communications asked whether the public could be shown the box without being invited to inspect it.

The Cabinet Secretary convened a Context Containment Board.

Its first decision was that context should be visible in principle, accessible in phases, and understandable in exceptional circumstances.

The junior adviser suggested publishing the facts plainly.

There was a silence long enough for several departments to identify savings.

## CLIMAX

The press conference began with the Context Box positioned proudly beside the lectern.

"Today, we are putting information where people can see it," the Prime Minister said.

The box displayed: VISIBILITY DOES NOT IMPLY COMPLETENESS.

A reporter asked what was missing.

Communications signalled confidence.

Policy signalled balance.

Treasury signalled affordability.

Legal signalled stop looking at the box.

The Prime Minister said the Context Box was a new era of accountable explanation.

The box displayed: ERA MAY REFER TO A PILOT PERIOD.

Another reporter asked whether the government had invented a way to add footnotes to optimism.

The Cabinet Secretary said optimism had always required responsible management.

Digital announced that future boxes would include filters, categories, and a public-facing summary of why the full context required specialist interpretation.

The junior adviser said the box had succeeded because everyone could now see exactly where the uncomfortable part was being kept.

The Prime Minister concluded that the National Context Box would help the country understand decisions in the round, in the aggregate, and where appropriate, at a respectful distance.

## TAG

By evening, departments had requested their own boxes.

Treasury approved a shared cardboard prototype, provided it could be reused for confidence, resilience, and any future inquiries.

Communications asked whether tomorrow's announcement could promise government outside the box.

Legal said only if the box was present.

**END**
